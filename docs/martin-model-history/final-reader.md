## Corrections Before Publication

### 1. D-28 Timeline Is Out Of Order

Priority: P2. Location: D-28, `Model Changes By Year`, JSON line 408 at review time.

The `December 1967 And 1968` D-28S row precedes the 1965 saddle and 1967 guard/neck rows. Move the D-28S row below 1967 and above April 9, 1968. This is especially noticeable now that chronological development is the page's purpose.

### 2. Small/Early Conflates Standard Strings With A Dated Construction Redesign

Priority: P2. Location: `1916 To 1926: Koa, Mahogany And Steel Strings`, JSON line 1544.

Current wording: `Style 18 adopted standard steel-string construction in 1923, followed by Style 28 in 1926.`

The research establishes when steel strings became standard, not a single complete construction redesign at those dates. The approved JVG prewar history separately notes that strengthening was gradual. Suggested replacement: `Steel strings became standard on Style 18 in 1923 and Style 28 in 1926.` The table already uses the supported wording.

### 3. D-35S And D-28S Measurements Need Their Evidence Scope Preserved

Priority: P2 evidence scope, not a demonstrated incorrect dimension. Locations: D-35 `1968: The D-35S Becomes A Regular Production Variant` and its short-scale FAQ (line 234 and following); D-28 `The 1950s And 1960s: The Postwar D-28 And D-28S` (line 424).

The new text presents the 1 7/8-inch nut and 25.4-inch scale as established late-1960s model specifications. The retained source notes explicitly limited those measurements to a documented 1968 D-28S and 1969 D-35S; they also rejected the D-35S dealer's 25.5-inch measurement as a nominal factory specification. These dimensions are plausible, but the supplied packets do not independently establish the new scope.

Resolve by using a period factory specification for both models, or narrow the copy to the supported 12-fret body and wider-neck distinction until such evidence is recorded. This does not require asking Joe. Do not use a modern reissue specification as a substitute for the late-1960s factory specification.

## Small Copy Improvements

### 4. State The D-28 Top-Material Change Directly

Priority: P3. Locations: table `1945 And The Later 1940s` and `1942 To 1947: Wartime Necks, Brace Carving And Herringbone` (lines 408 and 420).

`Spruce sourcing also changed in the postwar period` never tells the reader what replaced what. The packet supports a 1945-46 transition from red/Adirondack spruce to Sitka as the regular top material, with exceptions. Suggested concise wording: `During the 1945-46 transition, Sitka replaced red spruce as the regular top material; individual transitional guitars can differ.` This answers the user's what-and-when request without inventing a universal cutoff.

### 5. Remove Editorial Instructions From OM-45 Public Prose

Priority: P3. Locations: `1933: The Original OM-45 Run Ends` and `1934: Changes In The Successor Era` (lines 1281 and 1285).

Two sentences read like instructions to an editor rather than historical facts:

- `It also should not be increased by adding a disputed Deluxe total to it.`
- `They should not be inserted into the 1930 to 1933 OM-45 timeline as though regular OM-45 production continued through them.`

Keep the 40-guitar total without discussing how an editor should add it. For the successor paragraph, a direct replacement would be: `These changes belong to the succeeding 000 period, after original OM-45 production had ended.` The later comparison photo can then be identified plainly as a 1976 000-45.

## Specific Requested Checks Passed

- D-35 guards use a 1966-to-1967 transition in both table and prose; the draft does not force every guitar into a January 1 cutoff.
- D-45 uses **1939 as the standard inlay-pattern change**, preserves early custom exceptions, and does not assign hexagons to serial 73128. This accommodates Joe's ruling without contradicting the newly found specific 1938 custom example.
- D-45 writes **April 9, 1968** in full and distinguishes the revival from unchanged prewar construction.
- D-28 separates the 1938 brace-position move from the late-1944 brace-carving change and the later herringbone transition. Wartime reinforcement is correctly described as overlapping intervals.
- D-21 retains the six 1955 samples, 1956 public introduction, 1969 end, and qualified Indian-rosewood milestone. It does not claim every original D-21 was Brazilian rosewood.
- OM-18 and OM-28 distinguish guard and tuner transitions, then the 1934 name change from the later scale change. Modern comparison photos are labeled as such.
- OM-45 does not invent a Deluxe production count or a current survivor census; the snowflake description is explicitly photographic.
- Small/Early names affected sizes and styles instead of turning the entire family into one simultaneous specification change.
- Classical distinguishes G, C, 00-28C addition, and N development. It does not apply steel-string neck or bridge changes to every classical.
- Twelve-string uses transition wording for 1967 guards and does not invent an exact D12 bridge-plate serial cutoff.
- Tenor preserves the earlier 14-fret development and does not apply the general six-string 1934 date to that family.
- Plectrum distinguishes the 1931 annual total from total production, and manufacturing from later sales availability.
- Archtop preserves the C/R/F family distinctions, the R-18 pressed-to-carved change, and the disputed scale date is omitted.
- Electric distinguishes E, F and GT designs, avoids the conflicting F-55 count, and separates 1967 production ending from 1968 sales listings.

No other concrete factual contradiction or major scope failure was found in this read. After the narrow corrections above, the chronology copy is suitable for the planned publication checks.


## Resolution

All findings applied before publication: D-28 row order corrected; standard-string wording narrowed; OM-45 editorial instructions removed; D-28 red-spruce/Sitka transition stated; D-28S and D-35S specimen-only dimensions omitted from model-wide claims. A targeted catalog search did not establish a broader basis for those dimensions. GT bolt-on construction corroborated by first-hand photographs of both GT-70 and GT-75 neck plates in Marshall’s study.
