# Dreadnought Timeline Research

Prepared October 23, 2026 for the requested rewrite of D-21, D-28, D-35 and D-45. Research only; no shared article files edited. Coverage ends in 1969. Source URLs below are editorial records, not proposed public links.

## Recommended Editorial Shape

Lead with how the model developed. Put date and actual factory change in each table row. Follow with short chronological sections explaining the resulting configuration. Remove specimen serials and inspection instructions from the main timeline. Retain a small number of Joe's photographs as labeled illustrations of the era, without turning their individual details into factory cutoffs.

Avoid inventing an annual change where the specification stayed stable. A useful D-21 timeline will be shorter than the D-28 timeline. The D-35's initial design, later trim and neck changes, addition of the D-35S, bridge-plate transition and wood transition provide ample substance without filler.

## D-21 Suggested Timeline

| Date | Public-Facing Fact | Evidence / Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1955 | Six D-21 samples establish the new rosewood dreadnought. | HISTORY p.13, already supplied and cleared in user audit. |
| 1956 | Public introduction of the D-21, with rosewood back, sides, bridge and fingerboard and dark tortoise-pattern body binding. | HISTORY p.13. D21-FORD confirms the distinction from D-28 ebony fittings and lighter binding. |
| Late 1950s to early 1960s | The core combination remains a 14-fret rosewood dreadnought with a rosewood board and bridge, dot inlays and dark trim. | D21-1967 reproduces period-catalog description and Longworth. This is a stable era, not a new change. |
| 1965 | The bridge moves to a short drop-in saddle. | BRIDGES, shared factory chronology. |
| 1967 | Black body binding and pickguard replace the earlier tortoise-pattern material; square-tube neck reinforcement supersedes the T-bar. | HISTORY, FRETS; acknowledge transition rather than make a part-date authenticity verdict. |
| 1968 | Rosewood bridge plates begin replacing maple. | Use the April 9 factory adoption date, without a size claim. |
| Late 1969 | East Indian rosewood enters the D-21's final original production. Factory history identifies D-21 254498 as the first official Indian-rosewood guitar. | HISTORY p.13. This serial is a documented factory milestone and therefore differs from a random specimen row. Optional to keep number out of main table and use it in the explanatory paragraph. |
| 1969 | The original D-21 run ends. | D21-1967 quotes Longworth's chronology; D21-1969 and D21-FORD corroborate. No exact month established. |

Suggested sequence of sections: 1955 Samples And 1956 Introduction; The Original Rosewood Fittings; The 1965 Saddle Change; Black Trim And New Neck Reinforcement; Bridge Plates In 1968; The Final 1969 Production. A separate small paragraph can explain later D-21 naming without making modern variants the focus.

Do not propagate D21-1969's line calling 254498 the last Brazilian guitar. HISTORY calls it the first official Indian guitar. Do not propagate D21-FORD's all-Brazilian assertion.

## D-28 Suggested Timeline

| Date | Public-Facing Fact | Evidence / Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1931 | D-28 introduced in the original 12-fret dreadnought form, after the D-2 designation. | MUSEUM and S-ORDERS. |
| 1934 | Standard dreadnought design becomes the familiar 14-fret neck/body layout; the neck changes to T frets and steel T-bar reinforcement during the year. | MUSEUM, FRETS. Keep body redesign and neck reinforcement as separate developments within the year. |
| 1938 | X-brace crossing moves rearward, farther from the soundhole, while braces remain scalloped. | JVG-PREWAR. Scope to 14-fret dreadnoughts. |
| 1939 | The common 14-fret nut width narrows from 1 3/4 to 1 11/16 inches. | JVG-PREWAR, previously approved user audit. Describe a transition, not every instrument built in the calendar year. |
| 1942 through 1946 | Wartime neck reinforcement uses ebony during several production intervals instead of steel. | NECKS. Mixed periods mean no blanket all-years rule. |
| Very late 1944 | Scalloped braces give way to tapered profiles. | HISTORY p.12 and JVG-PREWAR. The approved 1,451 pre-change D-28 total can remain as supporting context. |
| Mid to late 1940s | Tapered bracing becomes progressively straighter. | JVG-PREWAR. Do not invent one exact day or serial cutoff. |
| 1945-46 transition | Sitka spruce replaces Adirondack/red spruce as the regular top material. | HISTORY p.12. Qualify as regular production; occasional later non-Sitka tops are a separate issue. |
| 1946-47 transition | Herringbone top trim gives way to black-and-white line purfling. | HISTORY pp.11-12 describes the final exception in 1947; current registry contains 1946/1947 conflict. Era wording preserves that overlap. |
| 1965 | Short drop-in saddle replaces the long through-cut saddle. | BRIDGES. |
| 1967 | Black guards and square-tube neck reinforcement enter standard production. | HISTORY, FRETS, JVG-DATING. |
| December 1967 / 1968 line | Regular D-28S production adds the long-body 12-fret design alongside the 14-fret D-28. | S-ORDERS records the December batch; HISTORY describes the regular-line addition as 1968. Early 1960s special orders preceded it. |
| April 9, 1968 | Rosewood starts replacing maple for bridge plates. | HISTORY; no universal same-day size change. |
| Late 1969 | Standard rosewood production changes from Brazilian to East Indian. | WOOD-FAQ; preserve overlapping-year qualification. |

Useful explanatory distinctions: 1938 brace position and late-1944 brace carving are different changes. Herringbone survived beyond the scalloped-brace era. The D-28S was a parallel 12-fret branch, not a replacement for the 14-fret D-28. Original 1931 12-fret D-28 and late-1960s D-28S should not be treated as the same production episode.

Omit a new exact dot-inlay year: the current manufacturer MUSEUM text says 1946; other references commonly say late 1944. A broad mid-1940s description is safer than silently choosing. The late-1930s diamonds-and-squares pattern and postwar dot pattern can be explained without an unsupported hard cutoff.

## D-35 Suggested Timeline

| Date | Public-Facing Fact | Evidence / Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1965 | D-35 introduced with a three-piece rosewood back, bound fingerboard and 1/4-inch top braces. The three panels let Martin use narrower pieces of rosewood. | STYLE35; corroborating original 1966 Folkway record already verified. These are model-defining features, not speculative tone claims. |
| 1965 design context | Short drop-in saddle belongs to the new model's introduction period. | STYLE35 and BRIDGES. Phrase as introduced during the saddle transition, not all examples guaranteed identical. |
| August 1966 | Mitered fingerboard-binding corners are discontinued. | STYLE35 explicitly dates this. Useful optional fine-detail milestone; no claimed serial number. |
| 1966-67 transition | Tortoise-pattern guard gives way to black; pale synthetic binding also changes material. | STYLE35 dates 1966, HISTORY 1967. Give the era range, or retain 1967 as standard while acknowledging transitional guitars. Avoid an exact binding-material cutoff without more primary documentation. |
| 1967 | Square steel tube replaces T-bar neck reinforcement. | FRETS and HISTORY. |
| January 1968 | D-35S enters regular production with a 12-fret design; the initial recorded regular batch contains 25 guitars. | S-ORDERS. Earlier individual or small orders exist, so do not call January 1968 the first-ever D-35S. |
| 1968 | Factory bridge-plate material changes from maple toward rosewood. | HISTORY's April9 date is general; STYLE35 contradicts it with 1966. Use factory change as context, not a guaranteed model-specific cutoff. |
| 1969 | Larger rosewood plates appear; standard body wood shifts toward East Indian rosewood. | STYLE35 for plate enlargement, WOOD-FAQ for standard body-material change. Exact enlargement boundary is not established here. |

The 1/4-inch braces remain an original D-35 feature throughout this span, rather than a later change. Avoid claiming that the three-piece back itself identifies the rosewood species. Describe the D-35S as the longer 12-fret body with a wider neck; the previously verified 1969 dealer measurement is a specimen measurement, not an all-years factory cutoff.

## D-45 Suggested Timeline

| Date | Public-Facing Fact | Evidence / Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1933 | First D-45 is the custom 12-fret, slotted-headstock guitar for Gene Autry. | D-45-FACTORY. Custom origin explains why the earliest guitars do not form one uniform template. |
| Mid-1930s | D-45 production remains small and custom, with both 12-fret and 14-fret variants. | D-45-FACTORY and HISTORY. Do not transplant the general D-28 1934 cutoff onto all D-45s. |
| 1938 | D-45 becomes a cataloged model after the earlier custom orders. | HISTORY p.12. This is useful chronology independent of the disputed inlay date. |
| 1939 | Hexagons become the standard D-45 fingerboard pattern in place of snowflakes. | Joe's explicit ruling, HISTORY p.13, D-45-GALLERY; STYLE45 says after 1938. See newly found special-example evidence below. |
| 1942 | Original production stops after a total of 91 guitars. | D-45-FACTORY and MUSEUM. |
| 1943 through 1967 | No regular factory D-45 production. | D-45-FACTORY states return in 1968. Pearl-decorated D-28 conversions from this gap are a different history. |
| 1968 | D-45 returns with the late-1960s construction platform, pearl trim and hexagon inlays. | D-45-FACTORY for return, D-45-GALLERY and D-45-INTERIOR for period appointments. Do not describe the return as an unchanged prewar reissue. |
| 1968-69 | Roughly 230 Brazilian-rosewood D-45s belong to the initial revived production. | Already cleared user audit: HISTORY 230 versus gallery 67 + 162 = 229. Preserve approximation. |
| Late 1969 transition | Standard rosewood production moves toward East Indian rosewood. | WOOD-FAQ. Avoid claiming every 1969 D-45 is Indian, or deriving a precise D-45 species boundary from the D-21 milestone. |

Explain prewar versus resumed construction in a compact comparison: early original run uses scalloped bracing, while the late 1960s factory comparison has non-scalloped braces, square neck reinforcement and a large rosewood plate. The 1968 factory plate-material change is a dated context paragraph, not a claim that all resumed D-45s had the same plate dimensions. Keep the full date April 9, 1968 wherever this event is mentioned.

### New Specific Inlay Evidence

D-45-NAMM, dated February 4, 2026, directly describes the original 1938 sunburst D-45 presented by its owner at the manufacturer's booth. Martin identifies it as the first D-45 with rear-shifted top bracing and hexagon inlays, as well as unusual F-9-style fingerboard stripes and tortoise headplate. This is stronger and more specific than the already known generic 1938 statement in the Holy Grail blog.

Keep Joe's 1939 general-transition ruling, but avoid the claims 'hexagons first appeared in 1939', 'no 1938 D-45 has hexagons', or 'every 1938 D-45 has snowflakes'. Recommended public text: 'Hexagon fingerboard inlays became the standard pattern in 1939. The earliest custom D-45s include exceptions to the later standard specifications.' A more explicit 1938 exception can be included if the parent considers it helpful. Source disagreement is now documented, not silently discarded.

Do not assign hexagons to serial 73128: its Guitarphile page does not identify its fingerboard pattern. Remove that specimen row entirely from the historical change table.

## Boundaries And Exclusions

- Do not invent a single uniform tuner schedule from the Folkway/Guitarphile examples; original dated tuners illustrate an era but do not establish a fleet-wide cutoff.
- No hard D-35 maple-to-rosewood plate date: STYLE35's 1966 conflicts with HISTORY's 1968. Do not use 1966.
- No new exact herringbone, dot-inlay or bridge-plate enlargement serial cutoff.
- No statement that all late 1969 rosewood instruments use one species or all D-21s are Brazilian.
- No claim that 1938 D-45 serial examples all share one inlay pattern.
- Dealer pages remain internal evidence under the user's new no-external-links direction.
