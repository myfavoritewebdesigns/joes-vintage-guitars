## Unique Information

Stock answer, in one line: Identify the cabinet and circuit family, corroborate tube-chart dates with component codes, distinguish reissues from originals, and document replacement parts.

Live results read:

- [How to date vintage Fender amps, Vintage & Rare](https://www.vintageandrare.com/blog/how-to-date-vintage-fender-amps/): Date-code tables, model-specific serial ranges and typical speakers.
- [Fender Bassman history](https://www.fender.com/articles/parts-and-accessories/going-low-the-history-of-the-bassman): Model chronology, cabinet changes, circuit transitions and reissues.
- [The Fender Bassman, GITEC](https://gitec-forum.de/wp-content/uploads/bassman_history_en.pdf): Circuit development, schematics and historical identification complications.
- [Fender Bassman, Gridleak](https://gridleak.app/versions/fender-bassman): Version specifications, component dating, originality checks and reissue differences.

What only this page has:

- “220947” and “All six digits are visible on a speaker in Joe’s 1959 Bassman.” The reviewer inspected the photograph and confirmed the transcription.
- “Joe’s board photograph contains newer blue service capacitors among older components.” The accompanying photograph supports the observation and shows why an original amp need not be untouched.
- “This designation is legible on Joe’s 1963 blonde Bassman chart. The faded ink stamp isn’t clear enough for a dependable two-letter reading, so no month is assigned from it.” The readable 6G6-B and indistinct date stamp are visible.

Enough to rank and be cited? Yes, as a photographic authentication reference. Competitors already cover many specifications and dating methods; the actual evidence behind “One speaker stamp doesn’t date the other three speakers or certify their cones” provides an attributable contribution. This is an editorial assessment, not a ranking prediction.
