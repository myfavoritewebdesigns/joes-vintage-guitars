## Required fixes

### 1. 000-28 identification: finish the scale-length explanation

**Location:** “Measure A 1934 000-28 Before Calling It Short Scale,” including the related FAQ.

The page correctly tells me how to measure from the nut to the twelfth fret and double the result. It repeatedly distinguishes long and short scale, but never gives either reference length. As an owner, I can follow the procedure and still cannot tell which group my result indicates. This matters because the companion chart assigns different ranges to the 1934 groups.

**Required change:** State the expected long- and short-scale reference lengths beside the measurement method, with appropriate wording about nominal published dimensions versus physical measurements. Retain the caution about replaced necks or fingerboards. This needs a sentence or small comparison, not another general warning or outbound link.

The historical distinction is supported by [Martin's museum account of the 1934 transition](https://www.martinguitar.com/blog-categories/from-the-factory/blog-061026-the-1933-martin-OM-45-that-went-to-war.html). The page's own cited [1934 serial 57851 research](https://theguitarphile.com/1934-martin-000-28-guitar-serial-number-57851/) explicitly gives the short scale as 24.9 inches. For terminology, Martin's current factory specifications identify the [OM-28's long scale as 25.4 inches](https://www.martinguitar.com/guitars/standard-series/OM-28.html) and the [000-28's short scale as 24.9 inches](https://www.martinguitar.com/10Y2500028.html). Do not silently treat current specifications as a complete historical specification sheet.

### 2. 15/17 identification: restore the missing 1950s in the 0-15 row

**Location:** “Original All-Mahogany Models And Related Instruments,” row `0-15 / 0-15H`.

The period cell says “1940s and early 1960s ordinary production; Hawaiian variant separately.” That reads as two separated periods and omits the 1950s. It conflicts with the prominently featured 1955 0-15 and the companion value chart's 1950–1959 row. An owner of a 1950s guitar could reasonably infer an unusual production gap where none is intended.

**Required change:** Say “1940s through the early 1960s ordinary production; Hawaiian variant separately,” or supply the verified start/end years. Preserve the distinction for the Hawaiian variant.

A contemporary primary source independently confirms the model was offered in the missing decade: the [1955 Carvin catalog, PDF page 14](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0476/5297/files/carvin_catalog_1955.pdf?v=1770818954) lists Martin's 0-15 Concert Size. The draft's 1955 serial attribution also agrees with [Martin's official year-end serial table](https://www.martinguitar.com/support-serial-number-lookup.html).

## Nonblocking observations

- **Style 21 identification caption:** The 1930 0-21 caption ends “Style 21 comparison where used with Style 28.” On this Style 21 page that sounds like an asset-use instruction left in the customer copy. Remove that final sentence here; the preceding description already identifies the guitar and its visible features.
- **Identification usefulness:** The 15/17 page's “Compare The Plain Appointments Carefully” section would be stronger with one compact, dated example of the actual rosette or marker differences. At present it often tells me what to inspect without saying what I should expect. This is an improvement, not a separate blocker, because the page supplies model stamps, photographs, and explicit references.
- **Style 21 value chart:** Consider marking the non-scalloped 1944 entries “late 1944” or adding a nearby transition note. The configuration column and missing-coverage explanation prevent a categorical error, but a year-first reader may overlook that the identification guide places the change late in 1944. Do not invent a price for the unlisted scalloped group.

## Checks and strengths

The six quoted serial readings—113035, 204220, 143500, 184931, 202105, and 266849—fall within the years claimed according to [Martin's factory table](https://www.martinguitar.com/support-serial-number-lookup.html). The corrected 1950 attribution is explained clearly. The oversized bridge, owner-reported 00-17 refinish, replacement NY tuners, and uncertain NY pickguard are disclosed in language an owner can understand.

The value pages consistently state the excellent/original/original-case benchmark and explain that their November date marks a calculation from annual guide ranges, rather than completed November sales. I found no conflicting figures between each page's highlighted ranges and its chart. I did not independently recompute every price from the annual book or conduct a new market appraisal.

**Release recommendation:** Apply the two small required fixes. The other four pages can ship on this content read; optional copy improvements should not trigger a broad rewrite.
