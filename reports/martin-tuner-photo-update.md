## Identification And Source Decisions

The Drive filename for request 2 is `1934-martin-c-2-10.jpg`, while Joe explicitly names a 1934 OM-28. An optional clarification was requested. In the absence of a correction, the displayed caption follows Joe’s direct identification. It does not infer an archtop conversion or claim the headstock photo proves a model. The neutral local filename preserves the distinction for later correction.

Request 11’s original filename identifies a D12-35; the visible caption uses Joe’s requested “1969 Martin 12-string.” Request 3 uses Joe’s “octagonal” terminology and the photograph’s visible eight-sided outline. The timeline uses “wide open-gear baseplates” rather than conflating this outline with Corwin’s general hexagonal-plate terminology.

The waffleback period is qualified, model-specific history, not a date assigned to the unidentified guitar in Joe’s existing detail photo. [Folkway’s inspected 1950 D-28](https://folkwaymusic.com/vintage-instruments/martin%20guitar/1950-martin-d28-0718) explicitly retains its original Kluson wafflebacks. [Galerie Casanova’s inspected 1957 D-28](https://www.galerie-casanova.com/en/produit/1957-martin-000-18-copie/) identifies Kluson K-500 wafflebacks; despite the URL slug, the page’s title and guitar description concern a 1957 D-28. [Corwin’s study](https://vintagemartin.com/tuners.html) dates the D-28 Rotomatic change to late 1957. No unsupported claim is made that all Style 18 or Style 28 models shared the D-28 schedule. These sources are text references only.

Captions identify only visible detail: the 1945 rear view cannot establish front bushings, the 1947 headstock cannot show the body joint, and the classical front view does not show complete side plates. Button material on the 1860s set is not inferred. Joe explicitly confirms originality on the 1965 D-28 and identifies the replaced buttons on the 1937 0-17.
