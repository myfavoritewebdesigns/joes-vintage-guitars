### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has NO FAQ section despite being a deep authentication guide — a clear gap. An FAQPage-schema accordion near the bottom (before "Final Thoughts") would capture long-tail authentication queries and qualify for FAQ rich results. Suggested Q/A (drawn from the page's own content):

1. **Q: Is a mismatched neck and body date on an early Precision Bass a problem?**
   A: No. A difference of under roughly twelve months between the pencil neck-heel date and the body-pocket stamp is normal factory practice — necks and bodies were built in separate batches and assembled when stock was on hand. A gap over ~12 months, or dates that conflict with the serial number, warrants scrutiny. *(Supported by VG-0051 — Fender neck date on the heel; and VG-0050/VG-0053/VG-0061 — Fender serial/date give a "no earlier than" floor, not an exact build date.)*

2. **Q: How do I tell an original early P-Bass finish from a refinish?**
   A: Look for tan lines under the pickguard, covers, and neck plate; nail holes punched through to bare wood; and aged butterscotch (not bright-white) blonde nitro. A uniform finish under the hardware with no tan lines suggests a refinish — which can cut collector value by 50% or more. *(VG-0104 — nitrocellulose; VG-0107 — refin = repainted after leaving factory; VG-0112 — originality is the dominant value driver.)*

3. **Q: Where is the serial number on a 1951–1953 Fender Precision Bass?**
   A: On the bridge plate, stamped into the chrome — NOT on the neck plate. The neck plate on these early instruments is plain and unmarked. *(VG-0066 — early-1950s Fenders carried serials on the bridge/cup/vibrato plate, moving to the neck plate around the mid-1950s — DIRECTLY corroborates the page; see §8 flags.)*

4. **Q: What is an early slab-body Precision Bass worth?**
   A: All-original examples in Excellent–Very Good condition have sold in the $20,000–$40,000+ range; modified player-grade examples run roughly $8,000–$15,000. A refinish or replaced neck/body is the single biggest value killer. Get a free appraisal before doing anything to the instrument. *(Frame as a range, estimates from completed sales — consistent with VG-0108; originality-dominant per VG-0112. Link the answer to /free-appraisal/.)*

**FLAG — needs a light verify (date precision, not a clear error):**
- **"Patent ... granted in 1952" / "Patent Pending" vs "Patent Applied For"** (lines 65, 149, 276) — the page states the Precision Bass patent was *granted in 1952*, with "Patent Applied For" before and "Patent Pending"/patent number after. The DB index has **no VG fact on the P-Bass patent date** to confirm or deny this. Historically the Fender Precision Bass design patent (Des. 169,062) was filed 1952 and granted 1953, and utility-patent timing differs — the "granted in 1952" claim is **plausibly off by a year and should be source-verified** before relying on it. Recommend Joe confirm the exact patent number + grant year. **Not contradicting any VG fact** (none exists), but it is the one date on the page worth checking.

**MISSING from the DB — solid facts on this page worth ADDING:**
- **VG-NEW (Fender):** "Early-1950s Fender Precision Basses (late 1951–1953, the slab-body era) used an early single-coil pickup with white string/cotton thread wrapped over the coil and yellow-and-black cloth-braid leads; DC resistance typically ~10k–13k Ω. This predates both the later rectangular single-coil and the 1957 split-coil humbucking P-Bass pickup." (Source: this page, lines 161–173; Joe Dampt firsthand.) — fills a P-Bass-specific gap; the DB has no Precision Bass pickup fact.
- **VG-NEW (Fender):** "On 1951–1953 slab-body Precision Basses the serial number is stamped on the bridge plate alongside a 'Patent Pending' stamp; the neck plate is plain/unmarked. Saddles are individual barrel-shaped pressed-fiber pieces (not metal); pots are typically Clarostat (not CTS); the tone cap is paper-in-oil." (Source: this page, lines 193–211, 290–296.) — extends VG-0066 with P-Bass-specific hardware/electronics tells. Note Clarostat-vs-CTS dating logic complements VG-0047/VG-0099/VG-0101 (EIA pot codes) — worth cross-referencing.
