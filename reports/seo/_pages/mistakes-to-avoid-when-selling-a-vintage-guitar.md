### 3. FAQ gaps

This post has **no FAQ section at all** and no FAQPage JSON-LD — a clear gap for a high-intent "how to sell" guide (sibling sell pages all carry FAQ schema). Adding a 4 to 6 question FAQ with FAQPage markup would add AEO surface and let it compete for "how do I sell my vintage guitar" voice/AI answers. Suggested Q/A drawn from the page's own topics:

- **Q: How do I find out what my vintage guitar is really worth?** A: Identify the exact make, model, and year (the serial number and neck/pot dates pin this down), then weigh originality, condition, and current market trends. The single biggest value driver is originality — an all-original instrument outranks a modified or refinished one of the same model. *(originality = dominant driver: VG-0112; refinish penalty ~−40%: VG-0074, VG-0107.)* For Fenders, note that the serial number alone gives only a "no earlier than" floor, not an exact year *(VG-0050, VG-0053, VG-0060).*
- **Q: Do I need the original paperwork to sell a vintage guitar?** A: It is not strictly required, but original receipts, a certificate of authenticity, factory paperwork, or luthier service records reassure buyers and justify a higher price — vintage guitars are frequently counterfeited or altered, so documented provenance reduces buyer risk. *(Drawn from the page's own Documentation section.)*
- **Q: Where is the best place to sell a vintage guitar?** A: eBay and Craigslist reach a wide audience but carry scam risk, listing fees, and local-meetup hassle; auction houses suit rare high-value pieces but are slow with no guaranteed sale price; a specialty dealer who knows the real value is usually the safest, fastest route. *(Page's own "Where to Sell" section.)*
- **Q: Should I accept the first offer on my guitar?** A: Usually not — resellers often open with a lowball to test you. Know your market value first; if an offer is not fair, decline. The right buyer comes along when the guitar is priced correctly. *(Page's own section.)*
- **Q: How should I ship a vintage guitar safely?** A: Use a sturdy hardshell case (or bubble-wrap and loosen the strings to reduce neck tension), pick a tracked carrier (UPS, FedEx, USPS, or DHL), and add value-based insurance to cover loss or damage. *(Page's own Safe-Shipping section; pairs with the "How to Pack a Guitar for Shipping" video — see §5.)*
- **Q: Does cleaning my guitar before selling actually increase the price?** A: Yes — the less work a buyer must do to get it into playing condition, the more they will pay. A careful clean and a fresh set of strings present the instrument at its best, but never over-polish or alter original finish, which can hurt value. *(Page's own Cleaning section; originality caveat VG-0112 / VG-0107.)*

No FAQ answer here requires a serial *range* claim, so VG flags are advisory context only — verify the originality/refinish framing against VG-0112/VG-0074 before publishing.
