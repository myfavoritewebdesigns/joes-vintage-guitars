### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has a strong existing FAQ (§faq). Gaps where a Q/A would help, by section:

- **§Headstock and Logo Decoder** lacks an FAQ. Suggested:
  - **Q: Did the Fender Jaguar ever have a spaghetti logo?** A: No. The Jaguar launched in 1962 after the spaghetti era ended, so it only ever wore the gold transition logo (1962 to mid-1968) or the black CBS "TV" logo (mid-1968 to 1975). Any Jaguar listed with a spaghetti logo has a non-original decal. *(supported by page body; no DB fact needed)*
  - **Q: How many patents should be on a vintage Jaguar headstock?** A: 3 in 1962, 4 in 1963-64, 5-6 in 1965-67, and counter-intuitively only 2 on the 1968-75 black TV logo. *(page body; cross-check note in §8)*

- **§Pot Codes and Dating** lacks an FAQ despite being the most search-relevant dating section. Suggested:
  - **Q: Can a pot code tell me exactly when my Jaguar was made?** A: No — a pot's EIA date code gives only the earliest possible build date; the guitar was assembled the same year or later. Read two pots and confirm they fall within ~8 weeks of the neck date. **VG-0048 / VG-0054 / VG-0100** (pot code = "no earlier than" floor).
  - **Q: What does a Fender pot code look like?** A: Three-digit maker code then year+week (e.g. 137 = CTS). **VG-0101 / VG-0102 / VG-0047.**

- **§Brazilian Versus Indian Rosewood** lacks an FAQ. Suggested:
  - **Q: Can I travel internationally with a Brazilian rosewood Jaguar?** A: Brazilian rosewood is CITES-restricted; confirm the species and check paperwork before crossing borders. *(page body — note the page's CITES "since 1992" claim, see §8 flag)*

### 8. DB fact check

Cross-checked the page's factual claims against VG-0001..VG-0112.

**Likely WRONG / contradicts a VG fact:**

- **CITES on Brazilian rosewood "since 1992"** (§Brazilian Versus Indian Rosewood: "CITES restrictions on Brazilian rosewood since 1992 affect international sales"). This conflates two different things. **VG-0109** explicitly states Martin did NOT stop using Brazilian rosewood because of CITES — the switch came in 1969-1970 from *supply shortages*, and Brazilian rosewood (Dalbergia nigra) was CITES Appendix I listed in **1992**. The page's "since 1992" date for the *Dalbergia nigra* listing is actually correct, but the framing is loose. **Verify:** D. nigra (the species used by Fender/Martin) was CITES Appendix I from 1992 — accurate. The broader Dalbergia genus (Appendix II) listing was 2017 and later relaxed for finished instruments in 2019. The page's statement is defensible but should specify it's the *species* listing of 1992, not a blanket "rosewood" ban. Low severity; tighten wording.

**Claims NOT in the DB but solid and worth ADDING:**

- **VG-NEW [Fender] Jaguar 24" scale length** — "The Fender Jaguar uses a 24-inch scale length, shorter than the 25.5-inch scale of the Stratocaster/Telecaster/Jazzmaster; the shorter scale lowers string tension." Heavily used across the page and the search-traffic hook. No existing VG covers Jaguar scale. Source: page body / Fender catalog.
- **VG-NEW [Fender] Jaguar production run 1962-1975** — "Fender produced the Jaguar from 1962 to 1975; CBS discontinued it in 1975." Core dating fact, repeated throughout, no VG covers it.
- **VG-NEW [Fender] Jaguar launch price $379.50 (1962, sunburst)** — page §Pre-CBS Years. Verifiable from period Fender price lists; useful value-context fact.
- **VG-NEW [Fender] Jaguar dual-circuit / strangle switch** — "The Jaguar has separate lead and rhythm circuits; the lead circuit's third slider engages a 0.003µF 'strangle' capacitor in series that rolls off bass." Distinctive, oft-searched spec. Source: Fender wiring.
- **VG-NEW [Fender] Jaguar slab→veneer fingerboard ~July 1962** — aligns with the Strat slab-board transition already implied by VG-0070; a Jaguar-specific entry would help. Source: page body.
- **VG-NEW [Fender] Jaguar four-bolt neck throughout 1962-1975** (never went to the 3-bolt/bullet-truss-rod the Strat/Tele got in the early '70s) — clean authentication shortcut from the FAQ; no VG covers it.
- **VG-NEW [Fender] Fender Fullerplast clear sealer (from ~1963)** — "Fender used Fullerplast, a clear chemically-cured sealer on bare alder from ~1963; its yellow-amber tone at chips/cavities is a refinish tell." Strong authentication fact, applies to all 1963+ Fenders, not just Jaguars. Worth adding generically.
