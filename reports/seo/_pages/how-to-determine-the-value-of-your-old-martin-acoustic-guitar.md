### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has **no FAQ section and no FAQPage JSON-LD** — a clear gap. Most other reference/value pages on this site carry an FAQ accordion with FAQPage schema; this high-traffic post (85 clicks) would gain AEO/featured-snippet eligibility from one. Suggested Q/A drawn from the page's own topic:

1. **Q: Where is the serial number on a Martin acoustic guitar?**
   A: On Martin flat-tops the serial number is stamped on the neck block inside the body, visible through the soundhole; from October 1930 the body size and style number were stamped just above it. *(VG-0077, VG-0078 — page currently says "seven-digit serial number on the sticker inside the soundhole," which is imprecise: it is **stamped on the neck block**, not a sticker — see §8 flag.)*

2. **Q: How can I tell if my Martin is pre-war?**
   A: "Pre-war" means built before WWII production changes (generally pre-1944, with 1939 also cited as a tone-spec cutoff). Look for forward-shifted scalloped X-bracing, herringbone purfling on a D-28, and an Adirondack/red-spruce top. *(VG-0079, VG-0084, VG-0075, VG-0076 — replaces the page's vague "carved top vs flat top" pre-war test, which is inaccurate; see §8.)*

3. **Q: What makes a pre-war Martin so much more valuable?**
   A: Rarity plus the most-prized construction: a herringbone, scalloped-braced pre-war D-28 is among the most valuable production flat-tops, and Martin halted D-45 production from 1942 to 1968, making pre-war D-45s exceptionally scarce. *(VG-0080, VG-0081.)*

4. **Q: Does refinishing or repair lower my Martin's value?**
   A: Originality is the single largest driver of vintage value — an all-original instrument outranks a comparable refinished or heavily repaired one. *(VG-0112; VG-0106/VG-0107 for the all-original / refin definitions.)* The page mentions condition but never covers originality, the dominant value factor — strong FAQ candidate.

### 8. DB fact check

Cross-checked the page's factual claims against the VG index. Several issues:

**FLAGS — likely WRONG / contradicts a VG fact:**

- **"Martin guitars have a seven-digit serial number on the sticker inside the soundhole."** — Imprecise/misleading. Per **VG-0077**, the Martin serial is **stamped on the neck block** (visible through the soundhole), not on a "sticker." Per **VG-0078**, the size/style number sits above it (since Oct 1930). Recommend rewording to "stamped on the neck block, visible through the soundhole." The "seven-digit" framing is also era-dependent (Martin's continuous sequential series since 1898 — **VG-0082** — was far fewer digits historically; seven digits only applies to modern serials).
- **Pre-war recognition test: "The 14-fret and 0000 models were first introduced … in 1934 and 1977 … the top of the 1934 model is carved, so it has a more complex design than the flat top of the 0000 model. Therefore the 1934 model is significantly more expensive."** — This is **garbled and unsupported by the DB**. The real pre-war tells are forward-shifted scalloped X-bracing, herringbone purfling, and Adirondack tops (**VG-0084, VG-0075, VG-0076**), and "pre-war" = pre-1944/1939 (**VG-0079**) — NOT a carved-vs-flat-top distinction. Martin flat-tops are flat-tops; the "carved top" claim looks like a conflation with archtops. The 14-fret neck arrived around 1929–1934 for various models, but the sentence as written is not a reliable pre-war identifier. **Recommend rewrite using VG-0079/0084/0075/0076.** High-priority correction — this is the page's core "how to recognize" instruction and it's wrong.
- **"a 20-fold price increase over the typical acoustic" / "the D-45 … is 20 times more valuable than the ordinary Martin acoustic"** — stated twice; not in the DB and reads as invented precision. The DB supports pre-war D-45 scarcity (**VG-0081**, 1942–1968 production gap) and the herringbone/scalloped pre-war premium (**VG-0080**) but gives no "20×" multiplier. Soften to "commands a large multiple" rather than a fabricated exact figure, or source it.
