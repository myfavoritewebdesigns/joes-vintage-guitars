### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has **no FAQ at all** and no FAQPage JSON-LD. Sibling guides (sell-fender/gibson, fender-SN) carry FAQ accordions with FAQPage schema, so this is a clear gap. Add a 4 to 6-question FAQ before the closing CTA. Suggested Q/A drawn from the page's own content:

- **Q: How can I tell if my 1956 Les Paul Goldtop is real?** A: Check the ink-stamped serial starting with "6," the no-wire ABR-1 bridge, P-90 soapbar pickups, the stepped control-cavity routing, and Bumblebee caps; originality of all parts is what matters most (VG-0106, VG-0112).
- **Q: What year did Gibson add the retainer wire to the ABR-1 bridge?** A: The "no-wire" ABR-1 is specific to the mid-to-late 1950s; Gibson added the saddle retainer wire in the early 1960s. *(Page claims this verbatim — see §8: NOT in the DB, candidate to add.)*
- **Q: Does a Goldtop's finish turning green mean it's fake?** A: No — the "bullion gold" nitro used real bronze powder that oxidizes green over decades; greening at arm-wear areas is a sign of an original finish, not a fake.
- **Q: What's the difference between a 1956 and 1957 Goldtop?** A: 1956 has P-90 single-coils; Gibson began fitting PAF humbuckers to the Goldtop during 1957 (VG-0041). Cross-link to the 1957 guide.
- **Q: How much is a 1956 Les Paul Goldtop worth?** A: Keep this range-based and route to a free appraisal — originality is the dominant value driver (VG-0112, VG-0108). *(Needs no fabricated figure; link to /free-appraisal/.)*
- **Q: Where is the serial number on a 1956 Les Paul?** A: Ink-stamped in (yellow) ink on the back of the headstock, prefixed "6"; never impressed into the wood in this era. Link to /how-to-read-gibson-serial-numbers/.

### 8. DB fact check

Cross-checked the page's factual claims against VG-0001..VG-0112.

**Consistent with DB (no conflict):**
- "17-degree headstock angle" for a true 1950s Gibson — matches **VG-0006** (Gibson reduced pitch from ~17° to ~14° around 1965). Correct.
- Long Tenon as a '50s hallmark — consistent with **VG-0004** (long neck tenon on '50s set-neck models). Correct.
- UV blacklight: original '50s nitro glows greenish — consistent with **VG-0017** (aged nitro fluoresces greenish/yellowish); the page's "purple or no reaction = refinish" caveat aligns with **VG-0018** (UV blind spots). Correct, well-hedged.
- Pot codes "137 (CTS) or 134 (Centralab) followed by 6 for the year" — matches **VG-0047 / VG-0101** (137=CTS, 134=Centralab). The "6 for the year" reading is consistent with **VG-0102** (single-digit year in the EIA code). Correct.
- P-90s in 1956, PAF arriving later — consistent with **VG-0041** (Gibson began fitting PAFs to the Goldtop during 1957). The page's "only full production year combining P-90 + ABR-1" framing does not contradict this. OK.
- Serial ink-stamped, not impressed, "6" + 4-or-5 digits — consistent with the ink-stamp solid-body era described in the Gibson SN guide / VG fact index (ink-stamp solid-body 1952–1960). OK.

**Minor wording flag (verify, not clearly wrong):**
- Captions describe the serial as a **"yellow ink stamp"** ("Gibson used a yellow ink stamp") while the body text says **"ink-stamped in black."** Internal inconsistency on the page itself (black vs yellow). Mid-'50s Gibson ink-stamp serials are commonly described as a darkish/black ink that can read yellowed-amber with age. Recommend reconciling the two captions to one description ("dark ink stamp that ages to an amber/yellow tone") so the page doesn't contradict itself. Not a DB conflict (DB doesn't specify the ink color), but a self-consistency fix.
