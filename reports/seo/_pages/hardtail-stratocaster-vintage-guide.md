### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has NO FAQPage / FAQ section at all — a clear gap for an Archetype-B SEO post. Recommended FAQ block (place before the "Sell Your Vintage Hardtail" CTA), with FAQPage JSON-LD:

| Q | A (source) |
|---|---|
| Are hardtail Stratocasters rare? | Yes. Historians and collectors estimate hardtails were only about 5% to 10% of total Stratocaster production in the 1950s and 60s; the figure rose to roughly 15% to 20% in some 1970s years before nearly vanishing in the 1980s. (page's own figures — see DB flag below) |
| Do hardtail Strats sound different from tremolo Strats? | Yes. Without the spring cavity and floating bridge, a hardtail delivers more sustain, a tighter low end, and a percussive "snap," trading the tremolo model's springy "ghost reverb" for a more fundamental, woody tone. (page body) |
| How can I tell a true factory hardtail from a "blocked" or "decked" tremolo? | A genuine factory hardtail has a solid bridge plate with no tremolo-arm hole and strings through the body via ferrules — no spring cavity routed in the back. A blocked/decked tremolo still has the cavity and arm hole. (page body) |
| What years were vintage hardtail Stratocasters made? | Hardtails were a catalog option from 1954 through the pre-CBS years and beyond; pre-CBS means built before CBS bought Fender in January 1965 (**VG-0049 / VG-0059 / VG-0072**). Note serials alone don't pin a single year (**VG-0050 / VG-0060 / VG-0061**) — date by neck date and features. |
| Are hardtail Stratocasters worth more or less than tremolo models? | Originality is the dominant value driver (**VG-0112**); a clean, all-original pre-CBS hardtail is rare and desirable, while refinishing knocks roughly 40% off value (**VG-0074**). For a specific figure, get a free appraisal. |
