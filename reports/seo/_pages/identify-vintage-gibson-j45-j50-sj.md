### 3. FAQ gaps

This page has **no FAQ and no FAQPage schema** — a real gap for a reference-style post that targets clear question-shaped queries ("how to tell a J-45 from a J-50," "what year is my Gibson Banner"). Add a short FAQ accordion before the Conclusion with FAQPage JSON-LD. Suggested Q/A:

1. **Q: What is the difference between a Gibson J-45 and a J-50?**
   A: They are nearly the same guitar — 16-inch round-shoulder body, mahogany back and sides, spruce top. The J-45 ships in a Sunburst finish (single-ply top binding); the J-50 is the Natural-finish version (usually triple-bound from 1950 on), which cost more because it required cosmetically flawless spruce.
2. **Q: How do I identify a Gibson Southern Jumbo (SJ)?**
   A: The dead giveaway is the fingerboard: an SJ has Double-Parallelogram inlays and a bound fingerboard with multi-ply body binding, where the J-45 and J-50 use simple dot inlays. A parallelogram-inlay guitar in a Natural finish is the Country Western (SJN), introduced in 1956.
3. **Q: What is a Gibson "Banner" headstock and why does it matter?**
   A: From 1942 to 1945, wartime Gibsons carried a gold headstock banner reading "Only a Gibson Is Good Enough." These Banner-era guitars (often built by the "Kalamazoo Gals") have light, hand-scalloped X-bracing and are the most collectible of the family. (Needs DB fact for the bracing/value claim — no exact VG-#### covers J-45 Banner bracing; see §8 "missing facts.")
4. **Q: My vintage Gibson has no model label inside — how do I date it?**
   A: Many 1940s-1960s Gibson flat-tops shipped with no interior label. Date them by physical features (bracing, bridge shape, fret count, logo style) and the Factory Order Number (FON) ink-stamped on the neck-joint block; from 1961 on, use the serial stamped on the back of the headstock. Note that Gibson serials from 1961 to 1969 are notoriously unreliable, so confirm with features [VG-0011, VG-0031, VG-0111]. (Link to `/how-to-read-gibson-serial-numbers/`.)

### 8. DB fact check

**Cross-checks against VG-0001..VG-0112** (note: the DB index is heavy on Gibson electrics and serial-dating; it has NO J-45/J-50/SJ-specific facts, so most claims here are uncheckable against it and are candidates for ADDING — see below):

- ✅ **Serial vs. feature dating** — Part 2's "Gibson FONs and serial numbers are not 100% consistent, these physical specs are your best tool" and Part 4's serial caveats align with **VG-0009, VG-0011, VG-0014, VG-0111** (1961-1969 serials unreliable; confirm year by physical features). Correct and well-framed.
- ✅ **FON is an ink-stamped batch number on an interior block** (Part 4) — matches **VG-0012, VG-0039** (FON = batch number ink-stamped into the wood, internal, separate from the serial). Correct.
- ⚠️ **FON letter-code years "'Z' is 1948, 'X' is 1950, 'S' is 1955"** (Part 4, line 146) — these are the well-documented post-war Gibson FON letter codes, but the DB's only FON-letter fact (**VG-0034**) covers the EARLIER 1935-1942 A-H scheme (A=1935…H=1942), NOT the 1947+ Z/Y/X… reverse-alphabet codes. So this claim is **not contradicted** by the DB, but it is **unverified by it**. The Z=1948 / X=1950 / S=1955 codes are accurate to standard references (the reverse sequence Y=1947, Z=1948, A=1949, B=1950… actually maps X to ~1953-54 in some charts) — **recommend a source-check on the exact letter→year mapping**, because published FON letter charts disagree on a year or two and "X=1950" vs "X=1953" is a real point of dispute. Flag for verification before relying on it.
- ✅ **Round-shoulder → square-shoulder transition ~1968-1969** (Part 2 §Square Shoulder) — consistent with the broad Gibson timeline; not in the DB but accurate to collector consensus.
- ⚠️ **"The '45' in J-45 literally stood for its 1942 retail price: $45.00"** (Part 3) — this is the standard, widely-repeated origin story and is very likely correct, but it is a frequently-cited "fact" worth a primary-source check (some sources frame the $45 as the introductory price rather than a strict naming derivation). Not in the DB; verify before treating as gospel.
- ✅ **"Kalamazoo Gals" built wartime Banner Gibsons** (Part 2, Part 3) — accurate to documented history (the John Thomas research). Not in the DB; safe.
- ⚠️ **"Before 1955, these guitars had 19 frets … In 1955, Gibson added a 20th fret"** (Part 3) — a commonly-cited dating tell, but the exact year varies by model in the literature. Accurate enough as a "quick glance" heuristic; recommend hedging the wording ("around 1955") rather than stating it as an exact, universal cutoff. Not in the DB.
