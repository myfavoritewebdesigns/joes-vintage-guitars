### 3. FAQ gaps

This page has **no FAQ section at all** and no FAQPage JSON-LD (unlike `/free-appraisal/` and the serial guides). A short FAQ would add AEO surface and FAQPage schema eligibility. Suggested Q/A drawn from the page's own topic:

1. **Q: How many Martin D-18E and D-28E guitars were made?**
   A: Very few. Martin produced the "E" series only in 1958–1959, with only a few hundred made total, which is why factory originals are rare and frequently faked. *(Needs a DB fact — see §8; this production claim is NOT yet in the DB index, flag VG-NEW.)*

2. **Q: How can I tell a real Martin D-28E from a converted D-28?**
   A: Look inside the soundhole for **ladder bracing** — factory E-series guitars are ladder-braced, while a standard D-18/D-28 with pickups added later is X-braced. Also check for the two pearl dots on a screwed-down bridge, a side-mounted output jack, and the "E" neck-block stamp.

3. **Q: Why is the D-28E worth so much more than a regular acoustic?**
   A: Beyond rarity, these are Brazilian rosewood, gold-hardware instruments, and the "Kurt Cobain effect" (Cobain's modified D-18E used on MTV Unplugged sold for ~$6M in 2020) pushed E-series values dramatically. *(The ~$6M / Cobain figure is on-page in spirit via the excerpt's "millions" claim but NOT stated in the body and NOT in the DB — see §8.)*

4. **Q: What's the difference between the D-18E and the D-28E?**
   A: The D-18E is mahogany with nickel/chrome hardware and a rosewood fretboard; the D-28E is Brazilian rosewood with gold-plated hardware, an ebony fretboard, white binding, and the checkerboard backstrip. Both share the dual DeArmond pickups and ladder bracing.
