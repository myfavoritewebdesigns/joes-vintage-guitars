### 3. FAQ gaps

The page HAS a 5-question FAQ (with FAQPage schema), well-matched to the topic. Gaps worth adding (the page's own facts support all answers; flagged where a DB fact backs the claim):

| Suggested Q | Suggested A | DB fact |
|---|---|---|
| What is herringbone, and why does it matter for a pre-war D-28? | Herringbone is the herringbone-pattern wood-marquetry purfling around the top of pre-war Style 28 Martins. Martin discontinued it on the D-28 in 1947 when the German-made trim became unavailable, so a herringbone, scalloped-braced pre-war D-28 is among the most valuable production flat-tops there is. | VG-0075, VG-0080, VG-0085/VG-0087 (herringbone discontinued 1947) |
| Why are pre-war Martins worth so much more? | Pre-war Martins (built before about 1944) have forward-shifted scalloped X-bracing and other construction features that collectors prize for their tone. Martin phased out scalloped top bracing in late 1944 as a wartime change, which is the line that separates the most desirable examples from the rest. | VG-0076, VG-0079, VG-0084, VG-0086 |
| Does the rosewood matter — Brazilian vs Indian? | Yes. Martin used Brazilian rosewood for back and sides through 1969 and switched to East Indian rosewood in 1969–1970 (a supply-driven change, NOT CITES). Pre-1969 Brazilian-rosewood examples carry a premium on D-28 and D-45 models. | VG-0088, VG-0089, VG-0109 |
| Where exactly is the serial number on a Martin? | It's stamped on the neck block inside the body, visible through the soundhole. From October 1930 the body size and style number were stamped just above the serial on the same block. Link: <a href="/martin-serial-and-model-numbers/">Martin serial number lookup</a>. | VG-0077, VG-0078 |

The herringbone and rosewood Qs are the strongest adds — they map to high-intent collector queries and reinforce the page's pre-war-premium thesis.

### 8. DB fact check

Cross-checked the page's factual claims against VG-0075–VG-0089, VG-0108, VG-0109, VG-0112.

**Claims that CHECK OUT against the DB:**
- "pre-war guitars (built before 1944)" / "A pre-war Martin is one built before 1944, the year Martin stopped scalloping its braces" → **consistent** with VG-0079 (pre-war = pre-1944) and VG-0086 (scalloped bracing phased out **late 1944**). Minor nuance: VG-0086 says late 1944, so "before 1944" as the pre-war line is the standard framing and fine, but the FAQ's "1944, the year Martin stopped scalloping" is technically *late* 1944 — acceptable, not wrong.
- "Martin stopped making the D-45 in 1942 and didn't build another until 1968, so there are no D-45s from that gap" → **correct**, matches VG-0081 exactly. Price table omitting any 1943–1967 D-45 row is consistent.
- "serial number stamped inside the body, on the neck block" → **correct**, VG-0077.
- "Martin invented the dreadnought in 1916" → not in the DB but historically accurate (Martin built the first dreadnoughts for Ditson in 1916). Solid; see MISSING below.
- Model value hierarchy D-45 > D-28 > D-18 and "older usually means more" → consistent with the general pre-war-premium facts (VG-0080) and VG-0112 (originality dominant). "A factory refinish cuts value by about half" is the Martin-side analog of VG-0074 (Fender refin ~−40%) / VG-0107 (refin = non-original) — reasonable, no Martin-specific refin percentage exists in the DB to contradict it.

**FLAGS / things to watch:**
- **No outright contradictions found.** The specific dollar ranges (e.g. pre-war D-45 $450k–$675k) are not in the DB to verify; VG-0080 only says "published values run very high" without a number, so the page's figures are unverified-but-not-contradicted. Keep them range-framed and dated (the page does: "Updated March 2026 … treat them as a guide").
- The FAQ answer "stopped scalloping its braces and made other wartime changes" pins it to "1944" — if you want it airtight, soften to "around 1944 (late 1944)" per VG-0086. Minor.

**Solid facts on THIS page that are MISSING from the DB and should be added:**
1. **"Martin invented the dreadnought in 1916."** Not in VG-0075–VG-0089. Worth adding as a new [Martin] fact: *Martin built the first dreadnought-size guitars in 1916 (originally for the Oliver Ditson Company); the dreadnought became a standard Martin catalog model in 1931 with the D-1/D-2 that became the D-18/D-28.* Source: Martin company history / the page copy + the table's 1931 start date.
2. **A Martin refinish penalty figure.** The page asserts "a factory refinish cuts value by about half" (~50%). The DB has Fender-specific refin penalties (VG-0074 ~−40%) and a general refin definition (VG-0107) but no Martin/acoustic-specific number. If verifiable, add a [Martin] fact for the acoustic refin penalty so future pages cite it consistently rather than restating ~50% un-sourced.
