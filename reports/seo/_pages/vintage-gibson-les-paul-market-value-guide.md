### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has a strong 8-Q FAQ already. Gaps where a Q/A would add coverage (and capture "people also ask" / AEO):

- **Section "The Les Paul Models" / Junior–Special** has no FAQ tying the cheaper models to the page. Suggested:
  - **Q: "Is a Les Paul Junior or Special a good vintage buy?"** A: Yes — they're the affordable way into a 1950s Gibson. A late-1950s Junior or Special with original P-90s and finish runs roughly $6,000 to $21,000 depending on year and color, with TV Yellow examples bringing the most. They were stripped-down student models, so there's less to "lose" to modifications, which makes an all-original one easier to find. *(values per the page's own `prices[]` table; originality framing VG-0112, VG-0106.)*
- **Section "What Changes the Value" / conversions** — the page lists modifications but never defines the collector term "conversion," which is a high-value Les Paul query. Suggested:
  - **Q: "What is a Goldtop 'conversion' and is it worth less?"** A: A conversion is a 1952 to 1956 Goldtop that's been refinished to sunburst and routed for humbuckers to imitate a 1958 to 1960 Burst. It's worth far less than a real Burst because the finish and routing are non-original, but a well-done conversion still commands a premium over an unmodified Goldtop because it delivers the Burst look and PAF tone. **[VG-0022]** *(conversion definition is a verified DB fact — answer should cite it.)*
- **Section "Vintage Les Paul Values…"** — no Q on the Burst production count, which is the single most-asked Les Paul fact. Suggested:
  - **Q: "How many 1958 to 1960 Les Paul Bursts were made?"** A: Gibson's shipping records point to roughly 1,500 to 1,700 sunburst Standards built from 1958 through 1960, which is why surviving original examples sell from about $200,000 to $450,000 and up. **[VG-0028 / VG-0029 / VG-0030]** *(needs DB facts — all three corroborate ~1,500–1,700 made and the Burst window mid-1958–1960.)*
- **Section "Selling Your Vintage Les Paul"** — no Q on the original case / documentation value. Suggested:
  - **Q: "Does the original case affect a vintage Les Paul's value?"** A: Yes. The original hard shell case (OHSC) and any paperwork or receipts add value and help confirm originality; the published value ranges on this page assume the original case is present. **[VG-0098]** *(OHSC definition is a verified fact.)*
