### 3. FAQ gaps

The page already has a strong 8-Q FAQ — but every question is StringTree-specific. It lacks any FAQ tying the topic back to Joe's own service or to neutral seller questions. Suggested additions (these also feed FAQPage schema and capture "where to sell" queries):

- **Q: What's the easiest way to sell a vintage guitar without listing it myself?**
  A: Sell it directly to a vintage dealer who buys outright. You send photos, get a number, and ship once you accept — no listing, no fees, no auction window. That's what Joe does every week out of the Phoenix shop. (Links → /free-appraisal/, /sell-a-guitar-collection/.)
- **Q: Does refinishing or replaced parts hurt what I can sell my vintage guitar for online?**
  A: Yes, significantly. Originality is the single largest driver of a vintage guitar's value (**VG-0112**); a refinish alone knocks roughly 40% off a vintage Fender (**VG-0074**), and an all-original instrument outranks an otherwise-identical modified one. Disclose mods honestly — undisclosed parts swaps are the #1 cause of "item not as described" returns. (Reinforces the §risks section with DB-backed facts.)
- **Q: Can I tell what year my guitar is from the serial number before I list it?**
  A: Only roughly. Most vintage Fender and Gibson serial numbers can't be pinned to a single year (**VG-0111, VG-0050, VG-0011**), and Fender amps generally can't be dated by serial at all (**VG-0052**) — you date them by tube-chart code. Use the serial guides plus physical features. (Links → the Gibson/Fender/amp serial guides; high internal-link value.)
- **Q: Where should I sell a vintage amplifier or effect pedal online?**
  A: Not the same place as a guitar. eBay's guitar/bass category is ~6.35%, but amps, pedals, and "everything else" sit at 13.25% — more than double. For vintage tube amps and rare pedals, a curated/zero-fee channel or a direct dealer sale usually nets more. (Links → /sell-an-amplifier-or-effect/; directly extends the page's own eBay-category point.)
