### 3. FAQ gaps

This page has NO FAQ section and NO FAQPage JSON-LD, unlike the sell/serial pages. Given its length and "authenticate this for me" intent, an FAQ accordion at the bottom (before the closing CTA) is the single biggest on-page SEO add — high AEO/featured-snippet value and a natural FAQPage schema target. Suggested Q/A (answers drawn from the page's own copy; VG cites where a DB fact backs the answer):

- **Q: What serial number range identifies a 1959 Gibson ES-335?** A: Roughly A28881 to A32285, hand-inked on the orange oval label, with the "A" (Artist) prefix Gibson used 1947 to early 1961. Note serial number alone is a starting point — confirm with physical features (VG-0009, VG-0011, VG-0014).
- **Q: How can I tell a real 1959 PAF pickup without removing the cover?** A: You can't fully verify without removing the cover, and you shouldn't — pulling the nickel cover hurts value. From the outside, look for the "Patent Applied For" decal on the baseplate and the era-correct nickel cover; leave forensic checks (L-shaped tooling marks, circle-in-a-square bobbin mold, white/zebra bobbins) to a specialist (VG-0003, VG-0026; bobbin-color timeline VG-0025).
- **Q: What is the "mummification" on 1959 Kluson tuner buttons?** A: The celluloid buttons off-gas over decades, turning amber, shrinking to a raisin texture, and eventually crumbling. Bright, smooth buttons on an otherwise-vintage '59 usually mean replacements (a Kluson stamp dates the tuner, not the guitar — VG-0002, single-line stamp era VG-0019).
- **Q: Are thin-top or thick-top 1959 ES-335s more valuable?** A: Thin-top '59s (1958-spec 3-ply construction that carried over) are generally slightly more desirable to tone purists; count the wood layers at the f-hole edge to check. Originality is the dominant value driver regardless (VG-0112).
