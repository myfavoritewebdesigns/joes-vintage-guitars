### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has **zero FAQ** and zero FAQPage JSON-LD — a clear gap for a high-intent authentication query page (siblings like sell-fender carry FAQ accordions + FAQPage schema). The page's own copy answers these already; surface 4 as an FAQ accordion at the end with FAQPage markup:

1. **Q: How do I tell a 1954 Les Paul Goldtop from a 1953 or 1955?**
   A: A genuine 1954 has a "4"-prefix ink-stamped serial, a 4° neck pitch (the wrap-tail bridge sits at proper height, posts not bottomed out), "no line" Kluson tuners, barrel/speed knobs, and Grey Tiger capacitors. A 1953 has the shallower 3° pitch; a 1955 brings single-line Kluson tuners and the start of bonnet knobs. *(All from the page; cross-check tuner transition vs VG-0019.)*

2. **Q: What capacitors should a real 1954 Goldtop have?**
   A: Sprague "Grey Tiger" caps (brown/tan, waxy, crayon-like). Bumblebee caps (black with colored stripes) appear in late 1955 to 1956, so finding Bumblebees inside means the guitar is re-capped or misdated to 1956 or later.

3. **Q: Is greening on the gold finish a sign of a fake?**
   A: No — the opposite. Gibson suspended real bronze powder in the lacquer, and that bronze oxidizes to a green discoloration at arm-wear and neck-contact points over decades. Authentic, wear-pattern-specific greening is expected on a 70-year-old example; a perfectly clean, no-checking goldtop is more suspicious.

4. **Q: Does a replaced bridge or tuners ruin a 1954's value?**
   A: It lowers value and must be disclosed, but it does not make the guitar a fake. A Tune-o-Matic conversion leaves plugged wrap-tail stud holes that confirm the original bridge. Originality is the single biggest value driver *(VG-0106, VG-0112)*, so an all-original example commands a substantial premium.

No FAQ answer here needs a DB fact that isn't already on the page; the VG citations above are corroboration, not gap-fills.

### 8. DB fact check

Cross-checked the page's factual claims against VG-0001..VG-0112. No outright contradictions found; a few notes and several solid facts MISSING from the DB worth adding.

**Consistent with DB (no action):**
- "no line" Kluson → single-line "Kluson Deluxe" stamp arriving ~1955 — consistent with **VG-0019** (single vertical "Kluson Deluxe" stamp from ~1956) and **VG-0002** (a Kluson stamp dates the tuner, not the guitar). ⚠️ Minor tension: the page says single-line arrived "around 1955," VG-0019 says "from roughly 1956." Both are within the documented transitional window; not a hard contradiction, but if the DB date is authoritative, soften the page to "1955 to 1956." **FLAG (soft).**
- Long-tenon neck joint = pre-1960s Kalamazoo construction, absent on 1969–70 reissues — consistent with **VG-0004**.
- Brazilian rosewood board, replaced by Indian "in the mid-1960s" — directionally consistent with the Martin-context facts **VG-0088 / VG-0089 / VG-0109** (Martin switched 1969–1970). The page's claim is about **Gibson**, and "mid-1960s" for Gibson is plausible/earlier than Martin; no DB Gibson-specific rosewood fact exists to contradict it. Not flagged, but see "missing facts."
- Nitrocellulose lacquer with checking/greening — consistent with **VG-0017** (aged nitro fluoresces green under UV) and **VG-0104** (nitro is thin solvent-based DuPont finish). The bronze-powder greening mechanism is more specific than anything in the DB.
- Pot codes "establish no earlier than" via Stackpole 304 / Centralab 134, XXYYWW format — consistent with **VG-0047, VG-0099, VG-0100, VG-0101, VG-0102** (304 = Stackpole, 134 = Centralab; code = maker + year + week; earliest-possible-build-date only). Strong match.
- Originality drives value; modifications must be disclosed — **VG-0106, VG-0112**.
- PAF humbuckers arrive 1957, replacing P-90 — **VG-0041** (Gibson began fitting PAFs to the Goldtop during 1957). Exact match.
- "conversion" / plugged stud holes context aligns with **VG-0022** (a 1952–1956 Goldtop modified, collector "conversion").

**No contradictions detected** on serial prefix ("4" = 1954), wrap-tail vs ABR-1 (late 1955), bullion-gold bronze powder, or the 4° neck pitch.
