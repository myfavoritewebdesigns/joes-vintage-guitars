### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has NO FAQ. A 3-4 Q FAQPage block before the closing CTA would add FAQPage schema (the rest of the site uses it heavily) and capture long-tail brass-nut queries. Suggested Q/A drawn from the page's own copy:

- **Q: Does a brass nut hurt a vintage guitar's value?** A: On a 1950s–60s "Golden Era" instrument it almost always does — the install often leaves headstock chips or an oversized slot, and it signals other modder changes (pickups, pots, wiring) that a buyer must re-verify. Originality is the single largest driver of vintage value (VG-0112). On a 1970s "player" guitar it matters far less.
- **Q: Does a brass nut actually increase sustain?** A: The nut only affects the tone of open strings; the moment you fret a note the nut is out of the signal path, so the "infinite sustain" feeling is largely psychological once you're playing leads. Brass does give open strings a brighter, more "fretted-note" timbre (zero-fret effect).
- **Q: Did any guitars come with a brass nut from the factory?** A: Yes — most famously Gretsch, which used plated brass nuts on some Chet Atkins 6120 models, finished to match the gold or chrome hardware. (Factory-original vs. aftermarket matters for value.)
- **Q: Should I replace a brass nut with bone?** A: For a pristine vintage piece, a period-correct bone or nylon nut is preferred and a brass swap is reversible — but factor in the cost of fixing any finish chips. For a 70s player you love, keeping the brass preserves that guitar's character.

No answer here requires a DB fact beyond VG-0112 (originality), already cited. The Gretsch factory-brass claim is NOT in the DB — see §8.
