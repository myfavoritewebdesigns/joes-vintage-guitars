### 3. FAQ gaps

The page already has a strong 12-question FAQ at the bottom. The gap is that the FAQ is monolithic at the END — there is no objection-handling near the high-intent **Norlin** and **serial** sections where readers form questions. Recommended additions (could go into the existing FAQ block or as 1-2 inline Q/As under the relevant section):

1. **§Serial — "Why do two of my Gibsons have the same serial number?"** A: Gibson serial numbers from roughly 1961 to 1969 are notoriously unreliable — numbers were duplicated, reused, and applied out of sequence, so the same number can appear on instruments from different years (e.g. the 100000–106099 range spans both 1963 and 1967). Confirm the year with physical-feature dating and pot codes, not the serial alone. **[VG-0011 / VG-0013 / VG-0031 / VG-0036]**
2. **§Serial — "Can I date a Gibson by serial number alone?"** A: No — for most pre-1977 Gibsons the serial gives at best a "no earlier than" floor; a confident year verdict requires corroborating physical features (volute, headstock pitch, logo, pot codes). **[VG-0009 / VG-0014 / VG-0111]**
3. **§Norlin — "What is a volute and does it hurt value?"** A: A volute is the carved triangular thickening on the back of the neck at the headstock joint, added by Gibson around 1969-1970 to reduce headstock breaks; it is a Norlin-era dating tell and is widely disliked aesthetically but is original to the guitar, not a defect. **[VG-0005 / VG-0008 / VG-0010 / VG-0046]**
4. **§Norlin — "Are Norlin-era Gibsons 'fake' or lesser instruments?"** A: No — they are genuine vintage Gibsons from the Norlin ownership period (1969-1986) with inconsistent but real quality; the best examples play and sound excellent and certain models (white/Silverburst Les Paul Custom, '70s Flying V) are actively collected. **[VG-0023]**

The existing FAQ already covers most-valuable year, refins, PAF, all-original, investment — no need to duplicate those.
