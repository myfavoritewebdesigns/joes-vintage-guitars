### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has **no FAQ and no FAQPage schema.** Sibling sell-pages (sell-my-fender/gibson) all carry FAQ accordions with FAQPage JSON-LD. Adding 3–4 Q/A here would (a) match the template, (b) win FAQ rich-result eligibility for a high-intent "selling" query, and (c) create natural anchor spots for the §1 links. Suggested:

- **Q: How old does a guitar have to be to be considered vintage?**
  A: The common rule of thumb is at least 30 years old — guitars from the 1980s, '70s, '60s and earlier. (Drawn straight from the page's "What Is A Vintage Guitar?" section. No DB fact needed; this is the site's own framing.)
- **Q: Do modifications lower a vintage guitar's value?**
  A: Yes. Originality is the single largest driver of value — replaced knobs, bridges, pickups, or tuners reduce what a collector will pay, even when they improve the sound. **(needs VG-0112)** — originality is the dominant value driver; also VG-0106 ("all-original") for the definition.
- **Q: Does a refinish hurt resale value?**
  A: Significantly. A refinished ("refin") vintage guitar typically loses a large share of its value versus an all-original finish. **(needs VG-0107** for the refin definition; **VG-0074** for the ~−40% Fender figure — keep the answer brand-neutral or cite the Fender study explicitly rather than implying it's universal.)
- **Q: What's the easiest way to sell a vintage guitar?**
  A: Selling directly to a specialist who appraises it in person (or from photos) is usually the fastest route to a fair offer without auction fees or shipping risk. (Page's own "Guitar Expert" bullet; natural link to `/free-appraisal/`. No DB fact needed.)

### 8. DB fact check

**FLAG — production-count claim looks WRONG / contradicts the DB.**

- Page claims (in "What Are The Most Valuable Vintage Guitars?"): *"Gibson's Cherry Sunburst Les Paul Standards, which was produced in the late 50s… only **650 were ever made**."*
  - The DB says the sunburst Les Paul Standard "Burst" was made **mid-1958 through 1960**, with **~1,700 made** and **~1,500–1,700 surviving** (VG-0028, VG-0029, VG-0030). **"650" contradicts VG-0028/0029.** The widely-cited shipping-total figure is ~1,700, not 650. Recommend correcting "650" → "roughly 1,700" (or "an estimated 1,500 to 1,700"), citing the shipping totals.
  - Secondary nit: the page says "produced in the late 50s." Per VG-0030 the Burst ran **mid-1958 through 1960** — "late '50s" is loosely OK but the model wasn't "Cherry Sunburst" until the finish change ~mid-1958 (VG-0044: gold → Cherry Sunburst ~mid-1958). Tightening to "1958 to 1960" is more accurate.
  - The **$280 original price → $400,000+ auction** framing is consistent with VG-0028's "$200k–500k+" range; not flagged, though "$400,000" is a point value where the DB carries a range — fine as a representative figure.

- Page claim: *"The guitar should be at least 30 years old to be considered vintage."* — This is a community rule of thumb, not in the DB and not a hard fact. No contradiction; leave as-is (it's framed as a guideline).

- Page claim: refinish "value will be significantly reduced." — Consistent with VG-0074 (Fender refin ~−40%) and VG-0107 (refin definition). Correct; no change needed. If §1's Fender link is added, the page can lean on VG-0073/0074 for specificity.

- Page claim: modified parts (knobs, bridges, pickups, tuners) "reduce the guitar's value… more original parts… the higher its value." — Consistent with VG-0112 (originality = largest value driver) and VG-0106 (all-original). Correct.

- Page claim: Fender custom colors "Sherwood Green" and "Candy Apple Red" in the '60s "to resemble the popularity of the American car industry." — Consistent with VG-0058 (custom colors drawn from DuPont **automotive** paints). Correct; the car-industry framing is accurate.
