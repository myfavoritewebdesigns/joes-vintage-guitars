### 3. FAQ gaps

The page HAS a 5-Q FAQ (`#faqs-collection`) but every question is generic single-guitar copy ("appraise MY guitar", "sell MY vintage guitar"). It has **no collection-specific or logistics questions** despite the whole page being about large multi-instrument buys. - **Q: How large a collection will you buy in one visit?** A: There's no upper limit — I've bought 700+ guitars and amps from a single retiring collector in one trip. For large collections I drive my own box truck or fly out and rent one on-site, with the goal of buying the entire collection in one visit and paying the same day. *(Backed by Notable Purchase #01 copy — no DB fact needed.)*
- **Q: Do you buy guitar collections from an estate or after a death in the family?** A: Yes. A lot of what I do is helping families and executors liquidate an inherited or estate collection. I'll appraise it remotely from photos first so there are no surprises, then come to you anywhere in the U.S. *(Targets "guitar estate sale" query directly.)*
- **Q: Will you buy amplifiers and other gear as part of the collection, not just guitars?** A: Absolutely — I buy basses, amplifiers, mandolins, banjos, and effects right alongside the guitars. One Southern Arizona buy was 100+ vintage Fender amps plus several guitars in a single day. *(Links naturally to /sell-an-amplifier-or-effect/.)*
- **Q: How do you decide what a whole collection is worth?** A: Each instrument is valued individually on make, model, year, condition, and originality, then totaled — **originality is the single biggest driver of a vintage instrument's value (VG-0112)**, so an all-original piece (VG-0106) is worth substantially more than a refinished one (VG-0107, ~−40% on average per VG-0074). Price-guide figures are estimates from completed sales, not firm quotes (VG-0108). *(Answer uses VG-0112 / VG-0106 / VG-0107 / VG-0074 / VG-0108 — cite when writing.)*
