### 3. FAQ gaps

The page has **no FAQPage / FAQ section at all** — a clear gap for an SEO/AEO blog post this long, and every other reference page on the site carries FAQ schema. A 4–6 Q FAQ near `#authenticating` (before the appraisal CTA) would capture "is my 1973 jazz bass worth anything / how to tell if real" long-tail and earn FAQPage rich results. Suggested Q/A drawn from the page's own content:

- **Q: How can I tell if a 1973 Fender Jazz Bass is real?** A: Cross-check the neck-plate serial against the documented 1972–1974 ranges, confirm a neck-heel date that lines up, read the CTS pot codes ("137" + a late-'72/'73 year-week), and verify period-correct poly finish, tuners, and pickguard screw pattern — no single tell is conclusive, they must tell one coherent story. *(serial/pot-code framing should cite **VG-0047** [137 = CTS], **VG-0054**/**VG-0100** [pot code = "no earlier than"], **VG-0050**/**VG-0061** [Fender serials approximate, neck plates batch-stamped]; the "1972–1974 serial range" claim is **not** in the DB — see §8.)*
- **Q: How much is a 1973 Fender Jazz Bass worth?** A: In honest, all-original condition roughly $4,000 to $7,500 depending on condition and originality, per the page's own side-by-side; originality is the dominant value driver, and a refinished or parts bass is worth markedly less. *(cite **VG-0112** originality drives value, **VG-0074** refin ≈ −40% — this strengthens the answer; value range itself is the article's claim, not a DB fact.)*
- **Q: Is the Vintera III Early '70s Jazz Bass as good as a real 1973?** A: Sonically and ergonomically it gets shockingly close (early-'70s "C" neck, period-voiced pickups, vintage-tall frets, 7.25" radius) at ~$1,499 street vs $4k–$7.5k for an original, but the original's 50+ years of played-in resonance and provenance can't be reissued.
- **Q: Why are 1973 Jazz Basses heavier than '60s ones?** A: CBS-era construction used heavier alder (occasionally ash) bodies and thick polyester finishes; 9 to 10+ lbs is common, though the era also produced lighter ~9-lb examples that players chase (Jim's weighs 9.01 lbs).
- **Q (optional): What is a "CBS-era" Fender?** A: An instrument built after CBS bought Fender in January 1965 — bigger headstocks, heavier bodies, thicker poly finishes. *(cite **VG-0049**/**VG-0059**/**VG-0072** — CBS purchase Jan 1965; this is a solid AEO answer well-supported by the DB.)*
