### 8. DB fact check

Cross-checked the page's factual claims against VG-0001..VG-0112.

**Consistent with DB (no conflict):**
- §6 Cherry Sunburst fading to "Tea Burst / Honey Burst" + "originality drives value" framing → consistent with **VG-0105** (sunburst definition), **VG-0107** (refin), **VG-0112** (originality is the #1 value driver). Good.
- §6 Natural/Ebony "treat as a refin until proven otherwise" → consistent with **VG-0106 / VG-0107 / VG-0112**.
- §5 Kluson "stamp dates the tuner, not the guitar" is implied by the "often worn or replaced" note → aligns with **VG-0002** (Kluson stamp dates the tuner, commonly replaced). Page could state this more explicitly but is not wrong.
- §3 Norlin "1969 ... quality control started to slip" → consistent with **VG-0023** (Norlin era 1969–1986).

**Potential conflicts / things to verify:**
- ⚠️ **§3 "sold Gibson to the Ecuadorian brewery conglomerate Norlin in 1969."** "Ecuadorian brewery" is the widely-repeated origin of Norlin's name (ECL = Ecuadorian Company Limited / Maurice Berlin), but the framing "brewery conglomerate" is loose. **VG-0023** only fixes the era (1969–1986 Norlin) and doesn't characterize ownership. NOT contradicted by the DB, but the "brewery" descriptor is a folk simplification — verify against a primary source before treating as settled. Low severity (color detail, not a dating fact).
- ⚠️ **§5 "the move from nickel to chrome plating around 1965."** Plausible and matches the general mid-60s Gibson hardware-transition window, but there is **no VG fact** confirming a specific 1965 ES-125 nickel→chrome tailpiece date. The DB does have **VG-0006** (Gibson dropped headstock pitch ~1965, a transitional year) corroborating 1965 as a real transition year generally. The specific tailpiece-plating claim is UNVERIFIED — not wrong, just not DB-backed. See "missing facts" below.
- ⚠️ **§1 "The guitar retailed at roughly $125 at introduction."** The "ES-number = price" convention is well-known lore but the exact $125 figure is UNVERIFIED against the DB. Reasonable, flag for sourcing.
- ✅ No conflict on serial reliability: the page does NOT over-claim serial precision (it routes dating to the Gibson SN page) — consistent with **VG-0011 / VG-0111**. Good; keep it that way in any new FAQ.

**Solid facts on THIS page that are MISSING from the DB and worth adding (with the page as a starting source — verify against Gibson literature/Duchossoir before committing):**
1. **ES-125 production run = 1941–1942 + 1946–1970** (one of the longest Gibson electric runs). Source: §1. → candidate new VG fact [Gibson].
2. **ES-125 used a laminated (pressed) body from introduction** — laminated maple top/back/sides settled by 1950; some 1946 examples are all-mahogany. Source: §3. → candidate VG fact.
3. **ES-125 suffix decoder: T=Thinline, D=Dual pickup, C=Cutaway, 3/4=short (22.75") scale** (e.g. TDC = thinline/dual/cutaway). Source: §2. → genuinely useful, model-specific identification fact missing from the DB.
4. **P-90 pole-piece transition: non-adjustable (1946–1949) → individually adjustable slot-head screws (1950 on).** Source: §4. → candidate VG fact [Gibson]; complements the existing PAF facts (VG-0003/0025/0026).
5. **ES-125 thinline (1.75") models introduced 1956; cutaway (Florentine) variants from 1960; full-depth cutaway (C/CD) from 1965–66.** Source: §2 table. → candidate VG fact.

The page cites Duchossoir, Carter, and Schultz/Beauchamp shipping data (§5) as its authorities — good provenance to verify these against before adding to the DB.
