## Fix before publication

### 1. Twin identification — “Twin Circuit And Hardware Comparison,” narrow-tweed row

The combined `5E8-A, 5F8-A` row says “three 12AY7/12AX7-family small tubes.” That statement collapses two different complements. The low-power 5E8-A uses four small-signal tubes, while the later 5F8-A uses three. Tube count is an identification check on this page, so a correct vintage 5E8-A could appear inconsistent with its own guide.

**Correction:** Split the cell by circuit, or split the complete row: “5E8-A: three 12AY7 plus one 12AX7; 5F8-A: one 12AY7 plus two 12AX7.” Confirm the final wording against the original circuit drawings during the technical check. Fender’s [’57 Twin owner’s manual](https://www.fmicassets.com/Damroot/Original/10001/OM_leg_gtramp_57_Twin_Amp.pdf), printed page 6, explicitly identifies its reproduced 5E8-A circuit and four preamp tubes, with three substitutable to the original-era 12AY7 complement. This is direct manufacturer support for the four-socket distinction; it should not be used to substitute modern output or weight figures for vintage specifications.

### 2. Twin value — “Twin Prices By Version,” paragraph immediately after the table

The table puts `1971 to 1972` in the no-master-volume group and starts the master-volume group at `1973`. The companion identification page says the factory master volume arrived in 1972. The prose currently tells an owner to put a master-volume amp in its appropriate row, without explaining what a factory 1972 master-volume owner should do. This is an internal reader-facing ambiguity even if every book row is copied correctly.

**Correction:** Preserve the verified book rows. Add a sentence explaining that their year buckets are valuation groupings rather than definitive introduction dates, and send a 1972 factory master-volume example for individual assessment instead of forcing it into a contradictory row. Link that sentence to the model-history section if useful.

### 3. Princeton value — “Princeton Values By Model And Period,” overlapping-entry instructions

The paragraph says to use the configuration column to choose the relevant row. A 1974 non-reverb Princeton with an AB1270 circuit and a tailed logo can still match both `1971 to 1979 / AB1270` and `1973 to 1975 / logo with tail`, with different estimates. Correctly documenting the configuration does not remove that overlap. “An overlapping entry isn’t a second value to add to the first” answers a question most owners will not have, while leaving the real selection problem unresolved.

**Correction:** Keep the source rows intact. State plainly that some source entries describe overlapping circuit and cosmetic groups, so more than one row may fit the same amp; where that happens, the chart does not establish a unique estimate and Joe should assess the specific example. Replace the instruction to choose by configuration with this limitation unless the source supplies a justified precedence rule.

### 4. Twin value — opening scope note or first chart preface

This page reaches the reissue/Tone Master exclusion only in the FAQ, despite the requested early original-or-reissue check. “Original models through 1980” establishes scope but does not help an inexperienced owner recognize a visually similar modern Twin before using the figures.

**Correction:** Add one short early instruction, such as: “These figures cover original vintage amps. If yours is a ’65 or ’68 Custom reissue, a ’57 Custom Twin-Amp or a Tone Master, use the identification guide before choosing a value row.” Link directly to the early reissue section. No lengthy duplicate reissue discussion is needed.

## Useful polish; not publication blockers

- **Princeton identification, “Princeton Photo Timeline,” 1966 caption:** “The archive’s ownership of these photographs doesn’t imply that unpictured components have been independently certified as original” sounds like internal review language. Replace it with a concrete limitation: “These views show the cabinet, controls and chart; internal originality requires chassis photographs.” Similar caveats recur in the Deluxe and Twin timelines; one concise statement per image group is enough.
- **Era page and model histories, repeated “house era” phrasing:** The dates are correct for the requested convention, but “house era,” “the site’s broad era label” and “in Joe’s house reference” foreground editorial machinery. Define the broad boundaries once, then use “the tweed era,” “the silverface period,” or “this guide’s date ranges.” Keep the model-specific continuation notes.
- **Princeton identification, specification table:** A short silverface non-reverb row or explicit pointer would complete the bridge from the AA964 non-reverb row to the later non-reverb entries in the value chart. The current table jumps to silverface Princeton Reverb although the guide covers both models through 1980. This is an improvement in completeness, not a reason to add speculative specifications.
- **All three value guides:** Several paragraphs repeat “photograph it,” “keep the repair record,” and “one feature does not prove originality.” The checklists already collect these actions. Trim a few repeated general cautions if tightening length, while retaining the distinctive details: the Twin’s speaker pair and tilt-back hardware, the Deluxe’s non-reverb/5E3 distinctions, and the Princeton’s speaker/baffle conversions.
- **Comparison, Vibrolux/Super value cells:** The deferred prices are acceptable for this PR and are not a missing-work finding. “Not listed here yet” reads slightly unfinished; “See individual appraisal” would be a more durable reader-facing placeholder if consistent with the user’s intended presentation. Do not add values in this PR.

## What works and should be retained

The value pages answer price intent immediately and distinguish estimates from offers. They handle recones, transformer replacements, recovering, accessories and service work as amplifier-specific condition issues rather than generic instrument grading. They give owners a realistic action even when the amp is untested: submit exterior photographs and the known history.

The identification pages lead with modern-model checks, avoid treating hand wiring as proof of age, and keep circuit designations separate from chart stamps. The MC, ML and PE examples are useful because they show what can actually be read; the 1957 Deluxe section properly refuses to invent a month. The original-versus-reissue distinction, surviving component evidence, and technician-only internal inspection form a coherent process.

The era guide preserves Tweed 1948–1959, Blonde/Brownface 1959–1963, Blackface 1963–1967 and Silverface 1967–1980 without pretending every model changed on the same day. The comparison page helps a player choose what to audition and what to measure; it does not promise a fixed result from wattage or faceplate color. No page recommends chassis serial numbers as a dating method, and no page improperly imports post-1980 models into its original-vintage price rows.

After the four focused fixes above, this set is editorially ready. No page warrants RETHINK.
