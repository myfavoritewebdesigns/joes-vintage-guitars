## Unique Information

Stock answer, in one line: Original prewar 000-42s can command substantial prices, but year, originality, repairs and provenance determine value; modern models and celebrity guitars require separate comparisons.

Live results read:

What only this page has, compared with that stock answer and those results:

- The coordinated 000-42 price table: 1918–1925, “$46,750 to $59,500”; 1930/1932, “$78,300 to $97,900”; 1934, “$97,450 to $121,800”; 1938, “$87,000 to $108,750”; early 1939, “$88,000 to $110,000”; late 1939, “$80,750 to $102,000”; 1940–1943, “$85,000 to $106,250.” This is a substantially more useful comparison format than isolated appraisals, subject to the problems below.

- The accompanying 0-42 table: 1890–1924 and 1926–1927, each “$18,700 to $25,500”; 1928–1942, “$31,150 to $40,050.” The explanation that the last band covers “intermittent production, not a claim that examples were made every year” prevents a misleading reading.
- A useful selection warning: “The two 00-42 groups overlap in 1927, and the 1939 000-42 rows require the actual nut width.” Neither the stock answer nor the initial price results supplies this practical chart-reading instruction.
- Specific first-hand archive material: “Joe’s 2012 000-42 comparison has two documented areas of minor binding separation and an added pickup.” This gives the condition discussion something observable from Joe’s own inventory.
- Two further archive examples: “1950 Martin 000-28: oversized replacement bridge documented in Joe’s listing” and “1936 Martin 00-21: bridge plate and adjacent braces.” Their value is instructional, and their different models are honestly identified.
- More useful inspection detail than the comparison pages: “Record the exterior bridge’s footprint, thickness, pin spacing and saddle arrangement” and “Worn pin holes, a small repair, a cap and a large replacement can have different consequences.” These help an owner obtain better evidence instead of merely hearing that condition matters.

Enough to rank and be cited? **Not yet.** The table and archive examples provide real information gain. But the most quotable material—the dollar figures—rests on “2026 annual price-guide ranges, adjusted downward,” without an identifiable source or explanation of the adjustment. The combined 1930/1932 configuration also appears unreliable. Correct and substantiate those elements, and the page would offer a useful consolidated answer beyond the results read.

## Fixes, In Order

1. Verify and separate the 1930 and 1932 configurations; remove the shared “Rare 14-fret examples” description unless instrument-specific evidence supports it.
2. Identify the annual price guide and relevant edition/pages, and explain what “adjusted downward” means and why that adjustment is appropriate.
3. Add a short, sourced comparison explaining how the guide-derived ranges relate to an actual vintage appraisal or sale, particularly where published examples exceed the table.
