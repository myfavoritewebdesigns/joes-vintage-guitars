## Unique Information

Stock answer, in one line: Date the serial, establish body size and period specifications, inspect pearl and construction, distinguish reissues from originals, and have a specialist assess alterations.

Live results read:

- [Understanding Martin Model Designations — One Man’s Guitar](https://onemanz.com/guitar/reviews/acoustic-guitars/martin/model-designation/): size, scale, appointments and modern model variations.
- [000-42 — Martin Guitar](https://www.martinguitar.com/guitars/standard-series/000-42.html): current factory specifications, including 24.9-inch scale, 14-fret joint and 1¾-inch nut.
- [Martin Style 42 Guitar Models — GuitarHQ](https://www.guitarhq.com/style42.html): historical specifications, production periods, body dimensions and illustrated examples.
- [1938 C.F. Martin 000-42 Guitar — Antiques Roadshow](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/roadshow/appraisals/1938-cf-martin-000-42-guitar/): an individual instrument, materials, history and dated appraisal figures.
- [Martin’s “12-Fret” 000-42 — George Gruhn](https://www.vintageguitar.com/15993/martins-12-fret-000-42/): a 1921 example, 25.4-inch scale, serial-only marking and conflicting early production records.

What only this page has:

- “The lead instrument here is Joe’s 2012 000-42, a modern comparison with documented binding separation and an added pickup.” This gives the photographed instrument an actual condition history, beyond a generic example of Style 42.
- “The model line says 000-42, and the serial below reads 1615472.” Paired with the stated year-end serial boundaries, this is a worked identification exercise using Joe’s photograph. The number is readable in the supplied image.
- “The dark pad covers part of the maker’s stamp above those readable lines.” This teaches a useful judgment: an obstruction and a valid date calculation are separate observations. The photograph supports the description.
- “Look at the thin, serif-style lettering in the model and number and the dark, burned-looking area around the logo.” The counterfeit comparison points to actual visible features and then limits the conclusion. None of the comparison results provides this particular illustrated inspection.
- “The 1950 000-28 bridge photograph shows a disclosed oversized replacement on a different model.” This supplies a concrete repair example and explicitly identifies its limits.
- “Page 284 groups those years under a 14-fret description, while Gruhn’s early-production discussion places a 1930 order in the 12-fret record.” This comparison turns conflicting references into a practical instruction: resolve the individual guitar before choosing a price group.
- “Each row receives one fixed editorial reduction of 11 to 15 percent, followed by rounding to the nearest $50.” This is unique methodology disclosure, although it is not original market evidence and adds little to authentication.

Enough to rank and be cited? **Yes, for the practical identification query.** “Read Joe’s pictured block in stages” and the concrete counterfeit and replacement-bridge comparisons give the reader something the historical summaries and factory specification pages do not. The page has a defensible reason to be cited for its illustrated inspection process. Its historical facts still properly depend on the named specialist sources.
