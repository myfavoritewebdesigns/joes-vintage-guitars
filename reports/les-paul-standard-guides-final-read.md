October 14, 2026. A separate reader reviewed the full built articles, including captions and FAQs, without loading the writer's brief or rule files. It compared live competing pages and returned **FIX FIRST for all three articles**. This is an editorial assessment of completeness and distinctiveness, not a ranking prediction. The report below preserves the material findings and records the implementer's disposition separately.

## 1958 Guide

Query: 1958 Gibson Les Paul Standard value and specs. Verdict: **FIX FIRST**.

The reader found the dated, condition-specific Goldtop value range and Joe's pictured 2011 R8 useful attributable material. It found phrases such as “For a sunburst comparison I use the Sunburst column” procedural rather than informative. Its strongest positive example was Joe's supplied judgment that a Burst commands significantly more than a comparable PAF Goldtop.

The material factual question is “247 Goldtops and 187 Sunbursts.” [True Vintage Guitar's 1958 guide](https://truevintageguitar.com/blogs/tvg-blog/1958-gibson-les-paul-standard-burst) states that the 434 total is not divided by finish. A census reproduced on [FUZZFACED](https://www.fuzzfaced.net/valore-les-paul.html) separately labels 187 as known 1958 examples. That coincidence raises a question, not proof that Joe's split came from that census. The reader could not corroborate the shipment split with an identifiable original ledger/table.

The reader also found “Neck Review: Profile, heel, headstock and any repair or reshaping history” an inadequate replacement for actual neck/fret specifications. It wanted one concrete original-versus-R8 construction comparison and a documented appraisal example behind the value judgment. Additional comparison sources were [GuitarHQ's original Burst reference](https://www.guitarhq.com/lpsun.html), [Gibson's 1958 retrospective reissue description](https://www.gibson.com/products/gibson-custom-1958-les-paul-standard-reissue-vos-iced-tea-burst), and [True Vintage Guitar's value guide](https://truevintageguitar.com/blogs/tvg-blog/how-much-is-a-gibson-les-paul-worth).

## 1959 Guide

Query: 1959 Les Paul Burst value and specs. Verdict: **FIX FIRST**.

The reader valued the pictured R8/R9 comparison, the documented R0 serial worked example and the precise cream-bobbin caption: “It shows one bobbin; the other remains hidden.” It judged the physical-photo interpretation more distinctive than repeated descriptions of an assessment process.

It wanted the original fret transition and early-year exceptions, a concrete provenance example behind “Provenance and original-owner photos can change my assessment,” a year-specific appraisal comparison, and an observable original-versus-R9 construction example beyond decoding the serial. It did not establish that the dated, scoped $200,000 to $500,000+ house range was erroneous.

The main comparisons were [True Vintage Guitar's documented 1959 acquisition](https://truevintageguitar.com/blogs/tvg-blog/this-1959-gibson-les-paul-standard-proves-that-theyre-still-out-there), [GuitarHQ](https://www.guitarhq.com/lpsun.html), and [True Vintage Guitar's value guide](https://truevintageguitar.com/blogs/tvg-blog/how-much-is-a-gibson-les-paul-worth). A separate [Guitar Value Guide result](https://www.guitarvalueguide.com/guitars/gibson/les-paul/1959) was inspected but its mixed original/reissue sales were not treated as reliable comparison evidence. The reader also noted that the related 1958 card inherits the disputed split.

## 1960 Guide

Query: 1960 Les Paul Standard value and neck changes. Verdict: **FIX FIRST**.

The reader judged this the strongest personal voice of the three, particularly “The slim neck is a turnoff for many players and collectors.” It identified the documented R0 serial and the vintage knob/tuner teaching sequence as useful distinctive material.

It found the later-1960 finish-development explanation missing and considered “The red dye can fade heavily” too broad without that distinction. It wanted a documented neck/profile comparison to make the depth-and-shoulders discussion concrete and to support the fuller-profile valuation judgment. It also suggested removing generic summaries such as “That gives the parts a place in the guitar's history.”

The comparison sources were [Guitar World's interview about an original 1960](https://www.guitarworld.com/gear/electric-guitars/1960-gibson-les-paul-standard), [Premier Guitar's 1960 example](https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/vintage-vault-1960-gibson-les-paul-standard), [Gibson's anniversary explanation reproduced by The Music Zoo](https://www.themusiczoo.com/blogs/news/50th-anniversary-gibson-1960-les-paul-standard), and [GuitarHQ](https://www.guitarhq.com/lpsun.html). The old Premier Guitar valuation was not used as a current price comparison.
