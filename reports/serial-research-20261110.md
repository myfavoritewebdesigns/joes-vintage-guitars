### Manufacturer Decoder Observations

Observed on November10,2026: Q11234 rejected; R11234 April1998; Y14716 November1998; 9846122 week46 of2098; ZA1234 replacement; JK123 November1970 only; 4C3401953Combo450; 4C62311956Combo450. These conflict with the contemporary records or omit a documented alternative. The article explains the relevant disagreement rather than treating one decoder as conclusive.

Exact exceptional results: V81=1958Capri325; V111=1958Capri310; 2LT125=1958 330F; 2LV126=1958 365F. V110/V112 and2LT127 were rejected. These are exact-record clues, not authentication or a continuous date range.

## Gretsch Findings

The existing overlapping vintage ranges, 1951 restart, misplaced1957 labels, later orange-label warning and guided label questions are preserved. The following gaps are addressed:

- One-digit vintage sequence example997 requires location context, since the same digits fit an early sequence.
- Revival codes allow one/two month digits, three/four model digits and variable sequence length. Published01319652 and999121447 now receive model-bearing readings. Missing separators preserve multiple plausible readings.
- Standard2003-onward prefixes remain tied to Gretsch's published list. Observed UC and IS prefixes receive qualified readings. CYG, CYGC and ISG receive factory-date confirmation guidance with no inferred year or month; their full markings are preserved.
- IWA suffixes and unverified extended import markings receive manual guidance, not invented factory identities.
- Model tables cover archtops, familiar hollowbodies, Jets, other solidbodies, basses, Japanese reissues, early Korean imports, Streamliner, Custom Shop and Roots/acoustic/folk instruments.
