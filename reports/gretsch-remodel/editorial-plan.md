## Corrections To Baseline Claims

- Hump-block markers are not exclusive to 1957. Manufacturer inlay history documents earlier high-end examples, some 1958 use and modern instruments.
- Thumbprints continued beyond the mid 1960s. A refret alone does not relocate inlays. T-Zone markers cannot prove a date without model/serial context.
- DynaSonic, HiLo'Tron, Filter'Tron and Super'Tron families recur in modern production. Pickup height is a setup choice, not an originality test. HiLo'Trons are visibly different from Filter'Trons, and the manufacturer dates introduction earlier than the old article's categorical 1960 boundary.
- A V-cut Bigsby describes the frame opening, not a V-shaped arm. Bridge/tailpiece replacements are common; preserve named types without claiming exact age from a movable component.
- Fred Gretsch label wording does not conclusively establish pre-Baldwin manufacture. Later orange/gray labels with 1/2-prefix sequences are not date coded. Missing interior labels alone do not prove fraud or a named 'ghost period'.
- The official sequential charts differ at boundaries. Return overlapping estimates and explicitly discuss 17622, the 1951 reset, 1957 labels reused in 1965/66 and numeric date-code ambiguity. Keep punctuation and full serial, do not strip arbitrary input into a plausible date.
- 'Made in USA' is an era clue, not proof of an exact ownership-change day. Hyphens also occur before unit numbers on Japanese revival guitars.
- 1985 family reacquisition and late-1980s production revival are different events. The Fender arrangement is licensing/management, not a purchase of Gretsch from the family. Correct Custom Shop factory/series overclaims and avoid claiming all modern pickups are TV Jones designs.
- Remove unsupported categorical value rankings, promises of top dollar, and claims that all model changes happened simultaneously. Preserve model families, construction distinctions, numbers, service path and diagnostic questions.
