What Goes Into My Offer

How Much Will You Pay for My Vintage Fender?

My offer reflects the guitar in front of me, not just a serial number. Model and year, original parts, finish and color, condition and repairs, the case and paperwork, and ownership history all help determine what I can pay.

Looking for an appraisal instead? Visit the Free Appraisal Page.

Model and Year

An early blackguard Telecaster or pre-CBS Stratocaster starts in a different place than a later CBS-era guitar.

Original Parts

Pickups, pots, wiring, tuners, bridge and neck plate matter. Keep every replaced part with the guitar.

Finish and Color

An original custom color, matching headstock or rare finish can make a major difference in value.

Read the custom color guide

Condition and Repairs

Play wear is expected. A refinish, routing or changed parts affect value, but do not automatically rule out a sale.

Case and Paperwork

Original cases, tags, receipts, bridge covers and repair records should stay with the instrument.

Ownership History

Family photographs and a clear original-owner story can add context and value that a serial number never will.

Selling directly to me gets you a cash offer with no listing, fees or wait. Other routes can bring more if the guitar sells, but they also add time, commissions and risk.

Sell Directly to JoeA cash offer, no public listing, no seller fees and help with the handoff.The offer sits below full retail because Joe takes on the work and risk of reselling.

Questions Fender Owners Ask Me

Before You Sell a Vintage Fender

You do not need to identify or clean the guitar before contacting me. Send what you know, and I will help with the rest.

Should I clean or repair my Fender before sending photos?

No. Leave it exactly as you found it. Cleaning, polishing, swapping parts or opening the guitar can remove clues I use to judge the finish and originality.

How can I tell how old my Fender is?

The serial number is a starting point, but neck dates, body dates, pot codes, pickup markings, hardware and construction details often matter just as much. Send photos and I will work through those clues.

Do you buy modified, refinished or player-grade Fenders?

Yes. A refinish, changed pickups, a refret or natural stage wear affects value, but it does not rule out a sale. Keep any original parts you still have with the guitar.

Does the original case increase the value?

It can. Original tweed, brown, white or black tolex cases, tags, receipts, bridge covers and replaced parts should all stay together with the guitar.

Can you appraise an inherited Fender from photographs?

Usually, yes. Clear photos of the front, back, headstock, neck plate or bridge serial number, controls and case are enough to begin. You do not need to know the model or year.

How does shipping work if I decide to sell?

We agree on the offer, payment and shipping details before the guitar moves. I arrange prepaid professional packing, fully insured FedEx shipping and tracking at no cost to you.

Do you buy Fender basses and amplifiers too?

Yes. I buy vintage Precision and Jazz Basses as well as collectible Fender tube amplifiers. Send the cabinet, chassis, transformer and speaker information you have.

Do I have to accept the offer?

No. Every appraisal and offer is free and carries no obligation. If the guitar is one I can buy, I will explain what I see and what I can pay.
