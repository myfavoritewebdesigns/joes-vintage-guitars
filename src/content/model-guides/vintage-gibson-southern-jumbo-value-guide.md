The Southern Jumbo, also called the Southerner Jumbo or SJ, spans wartime guitars, postwar round-shoulder instruments, square-shoulder models and the later SJ Deluxe. The letters “SJ” here mean Southern Jumbo; they do not mean the much larger SJ-200.

### How These Ranges Were Prepared

These are adjusted guide references based on [The Official Vintage Guitar Magazine Price Guide 2026](https://store.vintageguitar.com/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026.html), pages 201 to 202. Each source row was reduced by one fixed, randomly assigned percentage from 11% through 15%, applied to both endpoints. They are not reported sale prices or a statistical market average. Reviewed October 2026.

The baseline is an all-original instrument in excellent condition, with its original case where applicable. Repairs, replacement parts, refinishing and missing cases require individual assessment. The adjustment is already included; do not subtract it a second time.

## Match The Top, Finish And Body Shape

The Banner rows distinguish Adirondack spruce and mahogany tops. A Banner decal cannot select between those categories. The broad standard rows do not separately value rare rosewood-bodied wartime instruments; those need specialist comparison.

The overlapping 1962 rows reflect the guide’s round-shoulder and square-shoulder categories. Reference histories place the transition around 1962 to 1963. Use the body in front of you, plus its dating evidence, rather than treating January 1 as a universal specification change.

The 1954 to 1956 natural-option row covers the early natural SJ category. The [Country Western value guide](/vintage-gibson-country-western-value-guide/) covers the named Country Western/SJN and SJN Deluxe entries. Do not combine the two tables or choose whichever is higher for the same guitar.

## What A Refinish Changes

![Refinished 1946 Southern Jumbo showing split parallelogram inlays and a hand-painted replacement pickguard](../../assets/acoustic-models/1946-southern-jumbo-detail.jpg)

<figcaption>My Drive photograph of a 1946 SJ. The <a href="https://reverb.com/item/98162860-gibson-southern-jumbo-1946-natural">listing description</a> documents the refinish, replacement painted guard and cracks. Its price requires an individual condition assessment.</figcaption>

This guitar illustrates why a natural top is not proof of a Country Western. Its model identity and its present finish are separate questions. A refinish removes it from the table’s original-finish benchmark even when the instrument plays well.

Check the bridge height and footprint, neck angle, repaired cracks and interior braces. A recent setup cannot establish whether a bridge was shaved or the top was thinned during restoration.
