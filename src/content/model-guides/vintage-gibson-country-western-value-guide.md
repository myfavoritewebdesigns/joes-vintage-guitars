The Country Western belongs to the natural-finish Southern Jumbo family. Listings may call it Country Western, Country and Western, SJN or SJN Deluxe. Match the actual era and construction before selecting a range; a natural-finish J-50 or a stripped Southern Jumbo is a different comparison.

### How These Ranges Were Prepared

These are adjusted guide references based on [The Official Vintage Guitar Magazine Price Guide 2026](https://store.vintageguitar.com/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026.html), page 202. Each source row was reduced by one fixed, randomly assigned percentage from 11% through 15%, applied to both endpoints. They are not reported sale prices or a statistical market average. Reviewed October 2026.

The baseline is an all-original instrument in excellent condition, with its original case where applicable. Repairs, replacement parts, refinishing and missing cases require individual assessment. The adjustment is already included; do not subtract it a second time.

## Country Western, SJN And SJN Deluxe

The original natural SJ predates the Country Western name. The price guide groups the named Country Western/SJN from 1956, then lists the SJN Deluxe separately from 1970. Its 1954 to 1956 natural SJ precursor appears in my [Southern Jumbo value guide](/vintage-gibson-southern-jumbo-value-guide/).

The 1962 ranges overlap because the source separates body configurations. Identify round versus square shoulders and check the dating evidence; do not infer the body shape from a seller’s stated year alone. A repaired bridge or a replacement fingerboard can obscure the era clues.

## Original Natural Finish Or A Stripped Sunburst?

Inspect the edges, soundhole, neck heel and areas around the bridge and pickguard for evidence of refinishing. Finish appearance is one part of the examination; light, photography and aging can all change its apparent color. Do not scrape or remove finish to investigate.

## Condition Matters More Than The Listing Name

Natural-finish J-50s have simpler dot fingerboard markers, while the SJ/Country Western family uses split parallelograms. Later repairs, neck replacements and exceptions require more than one feature to settle the model. Use the [Country Western authentication guide](/gibson-country-western-authentication-guide/) before picking a price row.

Evaluate original finish, neck angle, bridge and saddle, cracks, brace repairs and the case together. A guitar that still needs a neck reset should not be valued as though that work is complete. Modern Sheryl Crow models and current reissues fall outside this vintage table.
