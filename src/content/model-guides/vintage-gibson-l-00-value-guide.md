### How These Ranges Were Prepared

These are adjusted guide references based on [The Official Vintage Guitar Magazine Price Guide 2026](https://store.vintageguitar.com/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026.html), page 169. Each source row was reduced by one fixed, randomly assigned percentage from 11% through 15%, applied to both endpoints. They are not reported sale prices or a statistical market average. Reviewed October 2026.

The baseline is an all-original instrument in excellent condition, with its original case where applicable. Repairs, replacement parts, refinishing and missing cases require individual assessment. The adjustment is already included; do not subtract it a second time.

## Why There Is One L-00 Price Band

The source provides one range for 1932 to 1946. I have not divided it into invented annual prices or assigned a new premium to every neck or finish variation. The introduction history and the guide’s pricing coverage are different questions: documented early L-00s predate 1932, and those need individual comparison.

Use my [L-00 authentication guide](/gibson-l-00-authentication-guide/) to separate the L-00 from the L-0, L-1, LG family and modern reissues. Black finish alone does not establish the model, and the zeroes in “L-00” do not mean it is an LG-0.

A completed neck reset and an unrepaired neck-angle problem are different conditions. Look at the heel fit, bridge height, saddle, top distortion and the work inside the body. Crack length alone cannot tell you whether a repair is stable or how much original material remains.

Lightly built small guitars can hide an oversized replacement bridge plate or brace work. These details can change the comparison even when the finish looks attractive. Photograph the interior if you can do so safely; do not force a mirror or camera against the top.
