## Before You Compare Your Fender

Start with the model, then match the year and configuration in its guide. I keep the condition assumptions beside the comparisons, so you can see how finish wear, repairs, changed parts and the case affect the reference you're using.

If you haven't established the year, my [Fender serial number and dating guide](/fender-guitars-serial-number-guide/) is a starting point. For a question about the finish, use my [Fender custom color authentication guide](/post/fender-custom-color-authentication-guide/).

You don't need to take the instrument apart before getting in touch. Keep its case, paperwork and any removed original parts together, and tell me about the changes you know of.

## Match The Year And Configuration

A Fender model name covers more than one construction period. Before choosing a range, compare the features named in the guide with the instrument you have. On a Precision Bass, the early slab body, later contours and split pickup distinguish separate groups. On a Jazz Bass, the control layout helps separate the early stack-knob version from later instruments.

Treat a serial number as one clue. A neck plate, pickup or neck can have been changed, so a plausible date still needs to agree with the rest of the instrument. If the features point to different periods, send photographs of each rather than choosing whichever date gives the highest comparison.

## Compare Finish, Repairs And Completeness

Original finish with playing wear and a later refinish are different conditions. A bright color alone doesn't establish a factory custom finish, and a clean-looking guitar can still have changed pickups or altered wiring. Describe what you know and leave uncertain details open for inspection.

Read the condition notes beside the model's figures. Some comparisons allow a quality refret or minor hardware changes; others assume more complete original parts. A replacement case, missing covers or repaired area needs to be considered within that specific group. There isn't one deduction that fits every Fender model and every repair.

## Prepare For An Individual Appraisal

Photograph the whole instrument in even light, then include the headstock, controls, bridge and any visible damage. Show the case inside and out. Old receipts, owner photographs and removed parts can help explain its history. Keep those items together, and tell me which details came from a previous owner. A guide narrows the comparison; an appraisal applies it to your guitar or bass.
