<h2 id="short-answer">The Short Answer</h2>

Johnny Marr didn't play just one guitar. He had a handful, and the searches tend to mash them together.

The cherry ES-355 is the one like the 1959 in these photos. Seymour Stein bought it for him in New York in 1984, and Marr wrote Heaven Knows I'm Miserable Now on it that afternoon. [Christie's](https://www.christies.com/en/events/marrs-guitars-the-johnny-marr-collection) has it estimated at £100,000 to £150,000.

The riff most people know first is This Charming Man, and that one is mostly a 1950s Telecaster that belonged to the producer, tracked with a Rickenbacker 330 of Marr's.

There's also a 1971 Martin D-28, a six-string, on There Is a Light That Never Goes Out. The Jaguar you see him with now is a modern Fender signature.

If you've got one of those models at home, the rest of this is for you: which guitar is which, and what a regular one actually trades for without the Christie's name on it.

<h2 id="es-355">The Cherry ES-355</h2>

I'll start with the 1959 in these photos, because that's the guitar I have in the shop this week.

In early 1984 Marr joked that The Smiths would sign to Sire if Seymour Stein bought him a guitar. Stein did. He picked up a cherry Gibson ES-355 on 48th Street in New York, and the [Christie's press kit](https://press.christies.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/6c7d2a4700a9977f095fab77370d60df.pdf) says Marr took it back to the hotel and wrote Heaven Knows I'm Miserable Now and Girl Afraid the same day.

That's the guitar in the sale, estimated at £100,000 to £150,000. Press copy calls it a 1960. The lot card says 1959-60 TDSV. A late 1959 and an early 1960 355 can look the same from across a room, so dating takes the orange label inside the bass f-hole and the factory order number. If you'd like that full walk-through, feel free to read my [1959 ES-355 guide](/post/1959-gibson-es-355-history-value/).

TDSV is the stereo Vari-tone version: two channels, a six-position rotary, and extra wiring in the body. The 1959 in these photos is a cherry mono, with four knobs, a toggle, and no rotary. Typically people pay more for mono 355s. In the case of Johnny Marr's guitar, the fact that it's stereo doesn't really affect the value, because so much of that number rides on the celebrity ownership.

<h2 id="charming-man">This Charming Man</h2>

Like the 1959 in these photos, the Stein 355 is the cherry Gibson. This Charming Man is a different guitar.

People call the intro "the Rickenbacker." [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/jun/22/johnny-marr-auction-guitars-smiths-this-charming-man-christies-london) said it when the sale was announced: the opening riff was played on a 1950s Telecaster. [Guitar World](https://www.guitarworld.com/features/johnny-marr-telecasters) has Marr saying the first Tele he ever used was the day he recorded that song, a '54, maybe a '53, that belonged to producer John Porter, tracked with a Rickenbacker. "The sound of that intro was always assumed that it was a Rickenbacker because that is what I was most known for at the time."

The 1982 Rickenbacker 330 in Jetglo is still on that record, and it's the look people remember. Marr bought it after the first publishing deal and used it on This Charming Man, What Difference Does It Make?, Still Ill, and Accept Yourself. He later lent it to Noel Gallagher, and it ended up on the cover of Oasis's Supersonic. Christie's has that 330 estimated at £60,000 to £80,000.

Porter's Tele isn't in the sale. The 1958 Telecaster in the photos below is the same model family, a few years later than the refinished '53 or '54 Marr remembered.

The green Tele that is in the sale is a third guitar. Roger Giffin built it around 1984, Angie bought it as an engagement gift, and it's the one on Top of the Pops in May 1984 and on Meat Is Murder. Estimate £20,000 to £30,000.

<h2 id="the-others">The Martin And The Jaguar</h2>

Two more guitars keep coming up in the same search.

The 1971 Martin D-28 is a six-string. Marr used it as the main Smiths acoustic on There Is a Light That Never Goes Out, Well I Wonder, and Cemetry Gates. Estimate £30,000 to £50,000. There's a 12-string in the sale too, a 1976 D-12-28, estimated at £4,000 to £6,000. They both say D-28 on the headstock, so the easy tell is counting the tuning pegs.

The Martin in the photos is a 1959 D-28, Brazilian rosewood and a bit earlier than Marr's 1971 Indian rosewood. Same shape, different wood, and a different number.

<figure>

![1959 Martin D-28 in a green-lined hardshell case, spruce top with a tortoise pickguard.](../../assets/blog/what-guitar-did-johnny-marr-play/1959-martin-d-28.jpg)

<figcaption>A 1959 Martin D-28 from the shop. Marr's main Smiths acoustic is a 1971 six-string D-28.</figcaption>

</figure>

The Jaguar people picture him with now is a Fender Johnny Marr Signature in Comet Sparkle, the one on No Time To Die, estimated at £8,000 to £12,000. Christie's press kit dates it 2017 and the [event page](https://www.christies.com/en/events/marrs-guitars-the-johnny-marr-collection) says 2018. Either way it's a modern signature. The vintage ones are the 1962-75 Jaguars.

The 1965 sunburst in the photos is that vintage family: offset body, rhythm circuit on the upper horn, claw pickups, floating trem. If you'd like more on those, feel free to read the [vintage Fender Jaguar guide](/post/vintage-fender-jaguar-guide/).

<h2 id="worth">What They Are Worth Without His Name On Them</h2>

Christie's numbers are what someone might pay for his instruments. Below is what a regular one of these actually trades for, from public asking and sold dealer pages. These aren't my offers, and they aren't a bid on your guitar. The market is thin, and asking usually runs ahead of sold.

A 1959 ES-355TD mono in cherry, all-original, sits about **$30,000 to $45,000** on my [1959 ES-355 page](/post/1959-gibson-es-355-history-value/). Stereo Vari-tone is a bit lower, roughly $22,000 to $38,000 on that same page.

A 1960s Rickenbacker 330 is asking about **$4,500 to $8,500** right now. Chicago Music Exchange had a 1966 Fireglo at $4,700, and Fretted Americana had a near-mint 1966 at $8,500. An 1980s 330 with no provenance is more like **$2,000 to $4,000**, and the comps there are thin, so I wouldn't price a 1982 like a 1966.

A 1960s Jaguar in sunburst is about **$4,000 to $8,000** off 1965 Reverb comps, with custom color running higher. That's the vintage guitar. The signature model is its own market.

An early-1970s D-28 in Indian rosewood is asking about **$3,500 to $7,500**. I wouldn't use the 1960-69 Brazilian D-28 table on that guitar, because the wood and the window are different.

Set next to those, the named lots in pounds are a different conversation: £100,000 to £150,000 for the 355, £60,000 to £80,000 for the 1982 330, £30,000 to £50,000 for the 1971 D-28, £20,000 to £30,000 for the Giffin, and £8,000 to £12,000 for the signature Jag. That's provenance money, which is a different thing from what a regular 1959 355 or a 1960s 330 trades for.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3 id="what-guitar-did-johnny-marr-play">What Guitar Did Johnny Marr Play?</h3>

He played several. The Stein cherry ES-355 is the one he wrote Heaven Knows I'm Miserable Now on. The 1982 Rickenbacker 330 is the early-Smiths 330. The 1971 Martin D-28 is the six-string acoustic on There Is a Light. The Jaguar most people picture now is his signature model. The green Giffin is the Top of the Pops Tele, and Charming Man used Porter's.

<h3 id="was-this-charming-man-played-on-a-rickenbacker">Was This Charming Man Played On A Rickenbacker?</h3>

Partly. The intro is mostly John Porter's 1950s Telecaster tracked with a Rickenbacker. Marr said so in Guitar World, and The Guardian repeated it when the sale was announced. The 1982 330 is on the record as well.

<h3 id="is-johnny-marrs-es-355-a-1959-or-a-1960">Is Johnny Marr's ES-355 A 1959 Or A 1960?</h3>

Christie's marketing says 1960, and the lot card says 1959-60 TDSV. I wouldn't pick one from a headline. The guitar in these photos is a 1959 cherry mono. His is the stereo Vari-tone configuration.

<h3 id="is-the-1971-d-28-a-12-string">Is The 1971 D-28 A 12-String?</h3>

No. The 1971 D-28 is a six-string. The 12-string in the sale is a 1976 D-12-28, a separate lot.

<h3 id="is-the-giffin-tele-the-charming-man-tele">Is The Giffin Tele The Charming Man Tele?</h3>

No. Charming Man used Porter's '53 or '54 Tele. The Giffin is a mid-80s one-off, the Top of the Pops guitar. Porter's Tele isn't in the sale.

<h3 id="what-is-a-vintage-es-355-worth-without-johnny-marr-provenance">What Is A Vintage ES-355 Worth Without Johnny Marr Provenance?</h3>

On my 1959 page, a clean original mono 1959 is about $30,000 to $45,000 in public asking, with stereo a bit lower. Christie's £100,000 to £150,000 is what his guitar might bring, which is a different market.
