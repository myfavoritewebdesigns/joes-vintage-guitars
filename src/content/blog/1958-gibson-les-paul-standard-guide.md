**A 1958 Les Paul can be a Goldtop or a sunburst, and those two versions belong in different price comparisons.** The finish changed during the year while the carved maple top, mahogany body, two PAF humbuckers and separate bridge and stopbar continued.

The full-guitar photo shows my **2011 R8 reissue, not a vintage 1958 example**. The close-ups below show vintage parts from my existing guides. Their captions identify the donor guitars where documented, including Goldtops, a Special and ES models.

## 1958 Les Paul Value

### How Much Is A 1958 Les Paul Worth?

My range for **1958 to 1960 Sunburst Standards is $200,000 to $500,000+**; **1957 to 1958 PAF Goldtops run $135,000 to $250,000**. These are US retail estimates for all-original guitars in excellent condition with the correct original case, reviewed in July 2026. My [vintage Les Paul value guide](/vintage-gibson-les-paul-market-value-guide/) explains the ranges and their scope.

Original finish and PAFs carry much of the collector value, while repairs, replaced parts and neck alterations can move a guitar outside those ranges. I also consider the neck's condition and documented provenance when choosing comparable guitars. A sunburst refinish on a Goldtop belongs in its own comparison, separate from a factory Burst.

## The 1958 Transition

Cherry Sunburst arrived in mid-1958, replacing the gold finish on this Les Paul model. The transparent center and shaded edge exposed the maple's grain and figure. Gold lacquer had concealed much of that appearance, so the finish change made the wood itself a larger part of the guitar's visual identity.

The humbuckers were already there. Gibson had moved from P-90s to PAFs during 1957, retaining the ABR-1 bridge and separate stopbar. The [1957 PAF Goldtop guide](/post/1957-les-paul-goldtop-guide/) shows that preceding configuration in detail.

There's a naming detail in my [Les Paul model chronology](/gibson-les-paul-dating-guide/): the **1958 catalog still calls it the Les Paul guitar or model, and the 1960 catalog is the first to say Les Paul Standard**. I use Standard here to identify the carved-top model people now search for under that name.

### Were All 1958 Les Paul Standards Sunbursts?

No. Gibson shipped both PAF Goldtops and Sunbursts in 1958. The change to Cherry Sunburst came during the year, while the carved maple top, two humbuckers, ABR-1 and stopbar continued. I separate these finish versions before comparing prices. The [Les Paul dating guide](/gibson-les-paul-dating-guide/) places both in the same model's development.

## 1958 Shipping Totals

### How Many 1958 Les Pauls Were Made?

Gibson shipped **247 Goldtops and 187 Sunbursts in 1958, for 434 total** in this Les Paul branch. The Burst-only figure is 187. These are shipment-ledger totals, not a count of guitars surviving today, and they exclude other Les Paul models such as the Junior and Custom. See my [Gibson shipping totals](/post/gibson-shipping-totals-1948-1979/).

That split explains why a general “1958 Les Paul” count needs a model and finish attached to it. For a sunburst comparison I use the Sunburst column; for a Goldtop I use the Goldtop column. Adding both describes the year's shipments across the finish transition.

## Neck And Frets

The usual 1958 neck description is full and rounded. Depth and shoulder shape both affect the feel, so I compare the actual profile instead of treating “1958 neck” as one fixed measurement. Two necks with similar depth can still sit differently in the hand.

The narrower fretwire associated with 1958 is another distinction from the wider wire commonly found in 1959. On a played guitar, surviving fret height and later fret work affect the experience as much as the model-year description. I look at the fret ends, binding and fingerboard together.

## PAF Pickups And Controls

### What Pickups Does A 1958 Les Paul Standard Have?

A 1958 Standard has two PAF humbuckers, whether it's a Goldtop or a Sunburst. Gibson introduced these pickups during 1957, so the 1958 finish change kept the humbucker layout already in use. PAF stands for Patent Applied For. My [PAF pickup identification guide](/post/gibson-paf-patent-number-pickup-guide/) explains their construction and the details beneath the covers.

Each humbucker has two coils. One has adjustable pole screws and the other has steel slugs, with a bar magnet beneath the bobbins. The covered pickup's underside exposes the baseplate, braided lead and familiar black decal. Those details are easier to understand in the Goldtop close-up below.

Each pickup has its own volume and tone control, and the three-way selector chooses the neck pickup, bridge pickup or both. The control-cavity photograph shows the striped Bumblebee capacitors alongside the wiring.

## Serial Numbers And Originality

A typical vintage 1958 solidbody serial is ink-stamped on the back of the headstock and begins with 8. I record the full number and its spacing, then compare the construction and model history. This is where a modern R8 can cause confusion.

Historic reissues use the common **M YNNN** format described in my [Les Paul dating guide](/gibson-les-paul-dating-guide/). M identifies the year being reissued: 8 for 1958, 9 for 1959 or 0 for 1960. Y is the last digit of the year the reissue was built, followed by its production sequence.

The examples show why I check the instrument and its paperwork together:

- A **2011 R8** reads **8 1xxx**: 8 identifies the 1958 design and 1 the build year's last digit.
- A **2022 R9** reads **9 2xxx**. That format looks just like a vintage 1959 serial.
- The **2014 R0's 0 4285** identifies a 1960 reissue, with 4 matching its documented 2014 build year.

The build-year digit repeats each decade. I use the paperwork and physical features to establish the decade; the serial's 4 alone doesn't distinguish 2014 from another year ending in 4.

### How Can I Tell A Real 1958 Les Paul From An R8?

I compare the serial with the guitar's construction, parts and paperwork. Both can have a number beginning with 8. On a Historic reissue, the second digit gives the build year's final digit, so a 2011 R8 reads 8 1xxx. My [Les Paul dating guide](/gibson-les-paul-dating-guide/) explains the physical checks that resolve the model and decade.

### My Short Originality Checklist

- **Finish And Wood:** Factory finish, touch-up or refinish; headstock, heel and body repairs.
- **Pickups And Controls:** PAF construction, coil repairs, component dates and solder history.
- **Neck And Hardware:** Neck reshaping, fret work, tuner holes and replaced bridge or plastics.
- **Records:** Serial, ownership history, case, receipts and retained parts that agree with the guitar.

My [Les Paul dating and identification guide](/gibson-les-paul-dating-guide/) covers the full inspection sequence. I keep the findings specific so a refret, a pickup rewind and a repaired headstock each get their own description.

For 1958, I also establish whether the guitar left the factory as a Goldtop or a Sunburst. A later sunburst refinish changes its finish history even when the body itself dates to the 1950s. That distinction carries through to the price comparison at the top of this guide.

## 1958 Vs. 1959 Vs. 1960 Les Paul Standards

### What's The Difference Between A 1958 And A 1959 Les Paul?

1958 includes both Goldtops and Sunbursts and is associated with a fuller neck and narrower frets. The 1959 Standard continues the sunburst design and commonly has wider frets. Both share the carved maple and mahogany construction, two PAFs, ABR-1 and stopbar. My [1959 Les Paul value and specs guide](/post/1959-gibson-les-paul-standard-guide/) covers the following year's details.

The next change is within 1960, when slimmer necks and later knob and tuner-button appointments enter the comparison. My [1960 Les Paul neck and hardware guide](/post/1960-gibson-les-paul-standard-guide/) shows those details with vintage-part photographs. Across these years, I compare the actual neck, finish and condition alongside the date.
